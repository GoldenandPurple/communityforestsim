# Classroom mechanics

How every student stays involved, whether the class is fifteen or thirty. This is the make-or-break layer. A
beautiful dashboard with a passive room is a failure. Everything here exists to force
participation structurally rather than hope for it.

## The room is the board, and everyone is themselves

You are not the teacher and not the lecturer. You chair the meeting: you put motions to the
floor, you call the votes, you keep order. Every student is a director of the community forest,
**deciding as themselves**. Nobody plays a Hillside resident, a logger, or a rider. The question
is always "what would *you* do?", which is the whole point of an ethics session. The dashboard
behind you is the single source of truth everyone watches.

## The community speaks through the screen, not through roles

The town is heard two ways:

- **The meters.** Recreation & Tourism, Hillside Residents, and the Forestry Sector are the
  community's support for the board. The directors watch them move; they do not act them out.
- **Public comments to the board.** Each year has two to four short comments from people in
  Cedar Bend (press **P**). Read them aloud, or let the room read them. Year 1's two Hillside
  comments are the knot: same street, opposite demands.

This keeps the market-segmentation lesson (three groups, conflicting needs) without asking
teenagers to wear a costume.

## The voiceless seat: the standing question

Before every vote, ask:

> **"Who does this affect who is not in this room?"**

It is under the public comments on screen, and on a poster for the front of the room (in the
printables). It stands in for everything that has no bar and no vote: the forest itself and the watershed, the people who will live on the
Hillside in fifty years, and the Nation whose territory this is. Give it a real pause. Any
director may raise a "long-view objection" before a vote is called, and the chair hears it.
This is the ethics of the whole simulation, and it sets up the Partnership year and the
80-year close.

## Voting

- **Big votes: private ballot first, then public hands.** A slip of paper (or phones, if the
  room allows and the teacher is fine with it) committed privately before the show of hands.
  This kills the follow-the-loud-kid effect and gives you a real number to enter on the
  dashboard. It also lets you show that the private vote and the public vote sometimes differ,
  which is its own quiet lesson.
- **Small/procedural calls: quick hands.** Do not ballot everything or you will run out of
  time.
- **You, the chair, enter the result** on the dashboard and apply the meter change live. Do not
  hand the laptop around.

## Managing energy

- **Plant a first voice.** Quietly ask one or two confident students to make their honest case
  early and strongly. A primed first voice breaks the silence and the rest follow.
- **Fast finishers / the minority report.** When the room settles quickly, ask a student or two
  to write the single-sentence case for why the board is about to make a mistake. Read a couple of
  these in the debrief.
- **Point at the comments.** "The mill worker says every year you don't harvest, their hours
  get cut. Does that change anything?" brings the town into the room without anyone playing it.

## Practice first

Before Year 1, run the two-minute practice vote (the logo for the forest trucks) exactly like a
real year: private ballot, enter the counts (V), hands, then press the winning letter. Nothing
moves. With fifteen students, every vote is visible, so the mechanics need to be smooth before
anything matters.

## The debrief

Open on what worked, not on what went wrong.

1. **What worked?** The screen shows everything the forest gave back. Ask two or three
   directors which decision they are proudest of, and why.
2. **So what?** Press Space to reveal each business concept. One sentence each, tied to a
   moment from their own game. End on the true story: this is not made up.
3. **Now what?** Exit ticket on a slip: one idea from this board they would use in their own
   business or town. Collect them at the door. Then the 80-year close.

## What to prep physically

- Ballot slips (or a decision on phones) for the big votes, plus one for the practice vote.
- Blank slips for the exit ticket.
- The standing-question poster, up at the front (in the printables).
- A way to gather the opening vote so it can be shown against the closing vote at the end.
- The laptop and projector, dashboard loaded and tested from the back of the room for
  legibility before the students arrive.
