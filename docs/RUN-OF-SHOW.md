# Run of show

A beat sheet for the 50-minute period. Budget roughly 42 minutes of live content
after students settle and before they file out. Times are targets, not a straitjacket.
The one rule if you run short: **protect Round 3.** It is the richest round and the one
the whole ethics arc turns on. Compress Round 2 or the epilogue before you touch it.

All meter changes referenced here are defined in `SCENARIOS.md`. The facilitator drives
every dashboard change with a key press (see `DASHBOARD-SPEC.md`).

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 0:00-0:03 | **Cold open** | No introduction. Point roughly at the mountain and ask: "There is roughly [$X] of timber on the lower slopes of Mount 7, right above Selkirk Hill. I am going to make you decide whether we cut it. But first: who owns it?" Let them answer. Most will be wrong, and being wrong out loud is engaging. Only after they have guessed do you give your one-line introduction. |
| 0:03-0:06 | **Setup** | You are the board of the Mount 7 Community Forest. Show the dashboard. Explain the four meters in under two minutes (if it takes longer, the dashboard is too complicated, fix the dashboard not the explanation). Assign the three room blocs and the voiceless seat (see `CLASSROOM-MECHANICS.md`). State the one rule: treasury hits zero and the forest is sold, game over. |
| 0:06-0:13 | **Round 1: The First Harvest** | The forest has to fund itself. How hard do you cut this year? Bloc caucus, floor debate, private ballot, public vote. Apply result live. Name the carry-forward consequence out loud. |
| 0:13-0:21 | **Round 2: The Windfall** | A salvage-logging opportunity or an outside buyout offer. Tests whether they trade the long game for fast money, and introduces "who captures the value" when an outsider wants in. |
| 0:21-0:31 | **Round 3: The Partnership** | The Ktunaxa and/or Secwépemc propose co-management. This is a real decision with real tradeoffs, not a footnote. It reframes the word "community": whose forest is it? Give it the most air. |
| 0:31-0:38 | **Round 4: Fire Season** | Their accumulated Forest Health / Wildfire Risk determines their exposure. The boards that preserved everything now face the highest fuel load above the homes. The past comes home and the meter does the moralizing. |
| 0:38-0:43 | **Epilogue and the reveal** | Read the "50 years later" outcome off their final meter state. Then show the opening vote next to the closing vote and let them see that the room changed its own mind. |
| 0:43-0:50 | **Debrief and close** | Name the business concepts they already used (do not lecture them first, cash them in after): profit vs break-even, who captures value, renewable vs non-renewable, social entrepreneurship. Then the 80-year close. Leave time for two or three real questions. |

## Facilitator notes

- **Keep the concept-naming short.** The moment the debrief feels like the lesson has
  "arrived," you lose them. Land each concept in a sentence and move.
- **Every reveal is a chance to move the meters visibly.** The reaction of the room to
  the dashboard reacting is a good chunk of the energy. Do not rush the apply-and-animate
  moment; let them watch the bar move.
- **If a bloc goes quiet**, call on it by its interest, not by name: "Bikers, this cut
  runs through your trails, are you really going to let that pass?"
- **Fast votes early, slower votes later.** Round 1 can move quickly. Rounds 3 and 4
  deserve the debate. Do not spend your debate budget on the easy round.
