# Run of show

There are two game lengths, picked with **G** on the dashboard's title screen:

- **Short game (4 years):** the original design, for one 50-minute period. The beat sheet
  directly below.
- **Full game (7 years):** adds The Trail Network, Where the Profit Goes, and The Mill or the
  Carbon. About 75 minutes, for a double block. See [Full game](#full-game-7-years-about-75-minutes)
  at the end.

## Short game (4 years, 50 minutes)

A beat sheet for the 50-minute period. Budget roughly 42 minutes of live content
after students settle and before they file out. Times are targets, not a straitjacket.
The one rule if you run short: **protect Round 3.** It is the richest round and the one
the whole ethics arc turns on. Compress Round 2 or the epilogue before you touch it.

All meter changes referenced here are defined in `SCENARIOS.md`. The facilitator drives
every dashboard change with a key press (see `DASHBOARD-SPEC.md`).

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 0:00-0:03 | **Cold open** | No introduction. Ask: "Above the town of Cedar Bend is Thunderhead Mountain. There is timber on its lower slopes, right above people's homes. I am going to make you decide whether we cut it. But first: who owns it?" Let them answer. Most will be wrong, and being wrong out loud is engaging. Only after they have guessed do you give your one-line introduction. |
| 0:03-0:06 | **Welcome and setup** | Welcome screen: the class has been selected as the corporate board of the Thunderhead Mountain Community Forest, owned by the Town of Cedar Bend; then, in a minute, what a community forest is. Then the setup screen. Lead with the green **Given back to Cedar Bend** tally: that is their real score, and half of everything above the reserve goes back to town each year. Explain the meters in under two minutes. Introduce the standing question, the voiceless seat (see `CLASSROOM-MECHANICS.md`). State the one rule: if the treasury hits zero, the Province takes the forest back, game over. |
| 0:06-0:08 | **Practice vote** | No stakes: pick the logo for the forest trucks. Run it exactly like a real year (ballot, V to enter, hands, press the letter) so the mechanics are learned before anything matters. |
| 0:08-0:14 | **Round 1: The First Harvest** | The forest has to fund itself. How hard do you cut this year? Public comments (P), floor debate, the standing question, private ballot, public vote. Apply result live. Name the carry-forward consequence out loud. |
| 0:14-0:21 | **Round 2: The Windfall** | Opens with **breaking news**: lumber prices hit a record (read the "this really happened" line aloud, then Space). Then a salvage-logging opportunity or an outside buyout offer. Tests whether they trade the long game for fast money, and introduces "who captures the value" when an outsider wants in. |
| 0:21-0:31 | **Round 3: The Partnership** | The First Nation whose territory includes the forest (unnamed, see `SCENARIOS.md`) invites the board into an equal partnership. This is a real decision with real tradeoffs, not a footnote. It reframes the word "community": whose forest is it? Give it the most air. |
| 0:31-0:37 | **Round 4: Fire Season** | Opens with breaking news: a wildfire prevention grant. Their accumulated Forest Health / Wildfire Risk determines their exposure. The boards that preserved everything now face the highest fuel load above the homes. The past comes home and the meter does the moralizing. |
| 0:37-0:41 | **Epilogue and the reveal** | Read the "50 years later" outcome and the given-back total. Then show the opening vote next to the closing vote and let them see whether the room changed its own mind. |
| 0:41-0:44 | **Debrief: What worked?** | Open on success, not failure. The screen shows everything the forest gave back. Ask two or three directors which decision they are proudest of, and why. |
| 0:44-0:48 | **Debrief: So what?** | Cash in the business concepts they already used, one sentence each, tied to a moment from their game (do not lecture them first): profit vs break-even, who captures value, renewable vs non-renewable, social entrepreneurship, partnership. End on the reveal that this is not made up (Golden's 2006 community forest allocation, lost in 2010, being tried again today). |
| 0:48-0:50 | **Debrief: Now what?** | Exit ticket on a slip: one idea from this board they would use in their own business or town. Collect at the door. Then the 80-year close. |

## Facilitator notes

- **Keep the concept-naming short.** The moment the debrief feels like the lesson has
  "arrived," you lose them. Land each concept in a sentence and move.
- **Every reveal is a chance to move the meters visibly.** The reaction of the room to
  the dashboard reacting is a good chunk of the energy. Do not rush the apply-and-animate
  moment; let them watch the bar move.
- **If the room goes quiet**, point at a public comment: "The mill worker says their hours get
  cut every year you don't harvest. Does that change anyone's vote?"
- **Let the milestones land.** When the given-back tally passes a milestone, a card names a real
  BC equivalent (a Zamboni, a salmon hatchery's water intake). Read it, enjoy it, then Space.
- **News is real, so say so.** Each breaking-news screen ends with a "this really happened" line.
  Reading it aloud is most of its value.
- **Fast votes early, slower votes later.** Round 1 can move quickly. Rounds 3 and 4
  deserve the debate. Do not spend your debate budget on the easy round.

## Full game (7 years, about 75 minutes)

The same arc with three extra years. The new years are quicker decisions (hands only, no
ballot, about 4 minutes each), so the debate budget still goes to The Partnership and Fire
Season. Budget about 67 minutes of live content.

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 0:00-0:03 | **Cold open** | As in the short game. Take the opening vote. |
| 0:03-0:07 | **Setup** | As in the short game. |
| 0:07-0:09 | **Practice vote** | As in the short game. |
| 0:09-0:14 | **Year 1: The First Harvest** | Ballot, then the Residents knot and a re-vote (U). |
| 0:14-0:21 | **Year 2: The Windfall** | The outside buyout. It will come back to bite them twice. |
| 0:21-0:26 | **Year 3: The Trail Network** | *Quick round.* Opens with breaking news: US lumber duties climb (a lean year, and the first community dividend usually lands here too). Charge for access (a paid shuttle and parking), build with the trail groups, or keep it a working forest. The pricing round: a forest sells more than timber. Optional 4 Ps drop-in here. |
| 0:26-0:37 | **Year 4: The Partnership** | Protect this. Most air of the session. |
| 0:37-0:42 | **Year 5: Where the Profit Goes** | *Quick round.* A good timber year leaves a surplus: community grants, a reserve fund, a FireSmart crew, or a Town capital project. Grants, the crew and the capital project all add to the given-back tally. Social enterprise in one question: who is the profit for? (The reserve quietly pays back in fire season.) |
| 0:42-0:49 | **Year 6: The Mill or the Carbon** | Opens with breaking news: a mill in the next valley closes. The mill needs a log contract; a carbon buyer pays the same to leave the trees standing. Carbon credits keep the forest dense above the homes and make thinning in Year 7 expensive. Let the Residents discover that. |
| 0:49-0:56 | **Year 7: Fire Season** | As in the short game, with seven years of choices behind the slider. |
| 0:56-1:01 | **Epilogue and the reveal** | Outcome and given-back total, then opening vote against closing vote. |
| 1:01-1:05 | **Debrief: What worked?** | As in the short game. |
| 1:05-1:11 | **Debrief: So what?** | The concepts, including pricing and revenue streams and where the profit goes. |
| 1:11-1:15 | **Debrief: Now what?** | Exit ticket, then the 80-year close. |

If you are running long, the first round to cut to a 2-minute hands vote is Where the Profit
Goes, then The Trail Network. Never compress The Partnership.
