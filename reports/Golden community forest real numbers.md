# Golden's lost 20,000 cubic metres anchor the game

The "roughly 4%" memory holds up. **In 2006 the BC forests minister allocated 20,000 m³ a year of timber for a Golden-area community forest. In 2010 the Province moved that volume to BC Timber Sales before any Community Forest Agreement was issued.** Against the Golden Timber Supply Area's allowable annual cut of **485,000 m³ (set June 3, 2010, and the newest figure found)**, 20,000 m³ is **4.1%**. The effort was revived as the **Kenpesq't Community Forest**. That is a partnership of the Shuswap Band, the Town of Golden and CSRD Area A, formalized in an MOU signed **November 21, 2022**. As of September 2026 it was not confirmed to hold a tenure. There is no Mount 7 Community Forest today. Most of Mount 7 is provincial Crown land, with Town-owned parcels and one private parcel on the lower slopes, and a woodlot licence, a timber sale licence and a small-scale permit already active on the mountain. Comparable BC community forests of this size bear out the game's placeholder dollar figures. Nakusp's 20,000 m³/yr forest paid its village **$250,000 in 2025**, and its dividends have ranged up to **$582,000**. So the recommended config mostly keeps the current numbers and ties each one to a real anchor. The two changes that matter are in the wording. Golden has not had a sawmill since the early 1970s, and Round 3 needs present-tense, rights-based language that does not have students play a real Nation.

## The 4% set-aside was real, lasted four years, and was never licensed

The Golden & Area Community Forest Initiative describes the history in one sentence. Efforts to bring a community forest to Golden date back to 1999. **"In 2006 the forest minister allocated 20,000 cubic metres of timber volume towards a community forest for the Golden area, but in 2010 the provincial government reallocated the 20,000 cubic metre volume to BC Timber Sales"** ([Golden & Area Community Forest Initiative](https://goldenareacf.com/about)). The same text appears in The Golden Star ([Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/bringing-a-community-forest-to-golden/)) and in the BC Community Forest Association's repost ([BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/bringing-a-community-forest-to-golden/)). Three outlets carry the same wording, so confidence is medium-high. No 2006 ministerial release or 2010 reallocation decision was found, though, and the article date is unknown.

The percentage comes from arithmetic, not from a source. The Golden TSA's AAC is **485,000 m³ as of June 3, 2010, "fully partitioned to conventional resources"** ([BC Gov, Golden TSA](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/timber-supply-review-and-allowable-annual-cut/allowable-annual-cut-timber-supply-areas/golden-tsa); [2010 apportionment report](https://canadacommons.ca/artifacts/29809008/golden-tsa-aac-apportionment-and-commitments/30708853/)). 20,000 ÷ 485,000 = 4.1%. The 2006 allocation was made against the pre-2010 AAC, which was not found, so "about 4%" is safer than "4.1%". Two further cautions apply. The Forest Act lets the chief forester postpone a new determination to at most 15 years after the last one ([BC Gov](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/timber-supply-review-and-allowable-annual-cut/allowable-annual-cut-timber-supply-areas)). A new Golden determination was therefore due around June 2025, and none was found. A June 2026 Golden Chamber event also presented a "New Forest Act" ([Golden Chamber](https://www.goldenchamber.bc.ca/2026/05/26/new-forest-act-june-2026/)). Check the Golden TSA page before quoting 485,000 m³ as current.

The story continues today. On **November 21, 2022**, the Shuswap Band, the Town of Golden and CSRD Area A (Rural Golden) signed an MOU at the Shuswap Band office near Invermere. In it they reaffirmed their intent to create the **Kenpesq't Community Forest "within the Golden Timber Supply Area"**, within Shuswap Band traditional territory ([Town of Golden](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest); [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/home/town-of-golden-celebrates-historic-moment-20-years-in-the-making/)). The Golden & Area Community Forest Team leads the work ([Town of Golden](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/golden-and-area-community-forest-initiative)). No source showed a provincial invitation to apply, a proposed volume or an issued licence. Community Forest Agreements are area-based, run 25–99 years, and are replaceable every 10 years ([BC Gov, CFAs](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/community-forest-agreements)). The Province is still inviting similar-sized community forests: in 2026 it invited McLeod Lake Mackenzie to expand by about 10,400 ha "which includes 20,000 cubic metres of wood fibre annually" ([BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0047-001063)). So a 20,000 m³ forest remains a realistic present-day size.

The history of the Golden TSA's licensees also bears on Round 2. The historic major licensees were **Louisiana-Pacific, Downie Timber and BC Timber Sales** ([TSR #4 data package](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/stewardship/forest-analysis-inventory/tsr-annual-allowable-cut/golden_tsa_data_package.pdf)). LP's forest licences passed to Pacific Woodtech in a sale that closed **August 1, 2022** ([BC Gov transfer notice](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/transfers/lp_to_pwt_transfer_info.pdf)). The real local lesson is that the community's volume went to a provincial seller, not to an outside company buying in. It makes a good debrief line for "who captures the value."

## Converting 20,000 m³ to dollars: about $10–13 a cubic metre before overhead

No source gives net revenue per cubic metre for a community forest, so the dollar conversion is an **estimate**, bounded by a few sourced figures. BC's Interior average stumpage is forecast at about **$13.83/m³ for 2026/27** ([BC First Quarterly Report, Sept 2026](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/quarterly-reports/2026-27_first_quarterly_sept_2026_web_secured.pdf)). Community forests pay discounted tabular stumpage instead, reported at **$18.10/m³ as of November 2021** in a snippet that was not checked ([BC Gov CFA page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/community-forest-agreements)). Low-grade wood loses money. Kaslo's community forest needed **$21/m³ from FESBC** just to break even on pulp logs ([KDCFS](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/september-2025-general-board-meeting-update/)). Delivered-log prices are published in the Interior Log Market Report, but its tables could not be read ([3-month report, Dec 2025](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-pricing/interior-timber-pricing/interior-log-market-reports/3mi_dec_25.pdf)).

The best check is outcomes at a forest of the same size. The **Nakusp and Area Community Forest (NACFOR)** is owned by the Village of Nakusp and covers **12,626 ha with an average harvest of 20,000 m³/yr**. That matches the Golden allocation almost exactly ([NACFOR](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/)). It paid the village **$250,000 in 2025** ([NACFOR](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/2025-dividend-to-village-of-nakusp/)) and **$465,000 in 2019**, and its record is **over $582,000 in 2014** ([Arrow Lakes News](https://arrowlakesnews.com/news/nacfor-2019-dividend-big-boost-to-nakusp-village-budget)). On top of that it gives about **$50,000 a year** in donations ([NACFOR](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/)). If about $300,000 reaches the community from 20,000 m³, then **net to the community runs about $12–15/m³ in an ordinary year**, after every cost. The comparable figures are volatile. The Sunshine Coast Community Forest went from **$1.2M net income in 2018 to a $391,700 loss in 2019** when cutting permits were not ready before fire season ([Coast Reporter](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/community-forest-posts-loss-hopes-to-resume-logging-in-fall-3415944)). Burns Lake's **53% margin in FY2020–21** came in a lumber-price boom and is not a normal year ([Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-comfor-reports-strong-fiscal-year-6309850)).

Here is the classroom conversion. **A full year's cut of 20,000 m³, after logging, hauling and stumpage, leaves roughly $250,000–$260,000 (about $13/m³). Fixed overhead of about $90,000 comes out of that, leaving about $170,000.** That sits inside Nakusp's documented dividend range. It is an estimate built to match real outcomes, not a published cost breakdown. No community forest's line-item operating costs could be read, so the $90,000 overhead figure is the weakest number in the model.

## Mount 7 is Crown land shared by several tenure holders

The land-status detail comes almost entirely from the Golden Cycling Club's **Mount 7 Trail Plan (November 22, 2023)**. Recreation Sites and Trails BC manages recreation on the mountain's Crown land under the Forest and Range Practices Act. The lower mountain "below 5K" (the 5 km mark on the Mount 7 Forest Service Road) holds **one private parcel and several parcels owned by the Town of Golden**. The private landowner allows the trails but bars machine-built construction. The area also has an **active woodlot licence, a Timber Sale Licence and a small-scale permit** ([GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)). The holders and locations of these tenures were not found. The honest answer to the game's title question, "Who owns Mount 7?", is **mostly the Province, partly the Town and one private owner, with timber rights already granted to others**. That fits a game about competing claims on one hillside.

The forest in the valley and on the lower slopes is mainly the **ICHmk1 subzone**, which occurs in the Rocky Mountain Trench between the Spillimacheen and Blaeberry rivers. Past wildfires left it largely **seral lodgepole pine, with Douglas-fir and western larch at low elevations** ([BC Ministry of Forests LMH20](https://www.for.gov.bc.ca/hfd/pubs/docs/lmh/LMH20-2.pdf)). The Golden TSA's main bark beetles are **Douglas-fir beetle, mountain pine beetle and spruce beetle**. Western balsam bark beetle attacked **11,985 ha in 2022** ([Golden TSA Forest Health Strategy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/forest-health/fh-strategies/goldenfhstrategy.pdf)). No Mount 7-specific stand or beetle data turned up.

Recreation is the mountain's clearest economic value. The GCC maintains **more than 180 km** of trails in four networks, Mount 7 among them ([GCC](https://goldencyclingclub.com/golden-cycling-club-trails/)). A study the GCC commissioned counted **just under 7,400 unique mountain bikers in summer 2023**, spending about **$5.8 million** directly. **61%** were overnight visitors and **45%** came from Alberta ([GCC economic impact report](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Summer-2023-Golden-Mountain-Biking-Economic-Impact-Final-Report-updated-May.pdf)). Mount 7 is also the launch for paragliding and for the Psychosis downhill race, which ran from 1998 to 2008 and returned in 2024 ([Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/places/mount-7)). The best-documented recent "harvest" on the mountain is fuel treatment. The Columbia Woodlot Association machine-mulched **11 ha** on Mount 7 ([e-Know](https://www.e-know.ca/regions/east-kootenay/woodlots-bc-to-lead-wildfire-risk-reduction-efforts/)). In June 2026 the Province awarded it **$271,500 for "Mount 7 Phase 2 Wildfire Risk Reduction Mulching"** ([BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0031-000669)). No public controversy over cutblocks on Mount 7 was found.

## Golden's fire record supports a credible Round 4, not a lurid one

Both local governments have wildfire plans, but neither could be opened. The CSRD says Community Wildfire Resiliency Plans were **completed for all its electoral areas by 2023**, Area A included ([CSRD](https://pub-csrd.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=46403)). The Town of Golden says it "has completed its Community Wildfire Resiliency Plan" and has done **over 30 FireSmart home assessments in 2024–2025** ([Town of Golden FireSmart](https://www.golden.ca/town-services/fire-emergency-services/golden-fire-department/firesmart)). Its January 2026 community profile says a **Wildfire Development Permit Area** is going into the new Official Community Plan ([Community Profile](https://www.golden.ca/sites/default/files/2026-03/Golden%20Community%20Profile_January%202026.pdf)). Hands-on work at **Selkirk Heights** is documented: BC Wildfire Service crews removed danger trees and surface fuels there over four June days under a Wildfire Prevention Program launched in 2012. The year is unconfirmed ([Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/wildfire-mitigation-project-comes-to-golden/)). Risk ratings and treatment hectares for Selkirk Hill are not verified. Many "Golden CWPP" search results are about Golden, Colorado, so check that any figure really concerns the BC town.

The local fire history gives each of the game's three fire outcomes a real counterpart. The **2024 Dogtooth fire** was found July 22 south of Golden. It grew to about **5,680 ha**, forced the evacuation of **100+ properties near Parson** and put **1,044** more on alert ([Town of Golden](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/news-notices/one-year-dogtooth-wildfire); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10643081/wildfire-golden-bc-evacuation-order-alert)). It destroyed **at least six homes on Rauch Road** ([My East Kootenay Now](https://www.myeastkootenaynow.com/37492/news/at-least-six-homes-destroyed-due-to-dogtooth-fsr-fire/)). The **2026 Sea Lion Mountain fire** was lightning-caused. It reached **1,144 ha** in steep terrain north-east of town with no threat to structures ([Eagle Valley News](https://eaglevalleynews.com/2026/08/14/bc-wildfire-service-issues-area-restriction-for-250-ha-blaze-north-of-golden-2/)). On Mount 7 itself, the only fire found was a **lightning start (N41279) that stayed at 0 ha** ([Town of Golden](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/news-notices/wildfire-n41279-kicking-horse-river-out)). These map neatly onto the game's "interface", "held" and "quiet" tiers. Dogtooth's damage came in rural interface south of town, not in the town core. The game's Selkirk Hill fire is therefore a plausible scenario, not a recreation of an event.

Treatment costs are sourced, but the figures are old. The Forest Practices Board put BC fuel treatments at about **$6,000/ha (2009 data)** ([FPB SIR43](https://www.bcfpb.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/SIR43-Fuel-Management-Update-1.pdf)). Williams Lake interface work cost **$2,618–$6,400/ha**, averaging **$3,406/ha** ([UBC Alex Fraser Research Forest](https://afrf-forestry.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2012/03/WUITreatmentBMPswAppendix.pdf)). Costs on steep ground like Mount 7 today are surely higher, but no current figure was sourced. The provincial FireSmart Community Funding & Supports stream gives high-risk communities **up to $300,000 a year** ([BC Gov FCFS](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/wildfire-status/prevention/funding-for-wildfire-prevention/crip/fcfs)). CSRD Areas A and B shared a **$182,400** Columbia Basin grant for FireSmart assessments and rebates ([Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/local-news/csrd-grant-supports-wildfire-preparedness/)). No per-hectare cost for prescribed burning in BC, and no burns near Golden, were found.

## Name the Ktunaxa and Secwépemc, and point to Kenpesq't as the real precedent

Golden institutions consistently name **two First Nations, the Ktunaxa and the Secwépemc, whose territory is unceded, and the Métis as having chosen the area as home**. The Town's draft OCP reads: "The Town of Golden is located on the traditional unceded territory of the Ktunaxa and Secwépemc peoples, the chosen home of Métis heritage and other diverse Indigenous peoples" ([OCP Bylaw 1516, 2026](https://www.golden.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/Bylaw%201516%202026%20Town%20of%20Golden%20Bylaw%20Official%20Community%20Plan%20PH.pdf)). School District 6, whose students will already know the form, says "the unceded lands of the Secwépemc and Ktunaxa People, and the chosen home of the Métis People of B.C." ([SD6](https://www.sd6.bc.ca/programs-and-services/indigenous-education)). Locally the Métis are represented by the **Métis Nation Columbia River Society** ([MNBC](https://www.mnbc.ca/citizens-culture/chartered-communities/metis-nation-columbia-river-society)). The Secwépemc community most involved around Golden is the **Shuswap Band (Kenpesq't)**, based near Invermere. It says it "has lived in the area around Golden and the Columbia Valley since time immemorial" ([Shuswap Band](https://www.shuswapband.net/about-shuswap-band/)). "Kenpesq't" is the Band's name in Secwepemctsín ([Town of Golden](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest)). The Ktunaxa Nation Council represents four Ktunaxa First Nations. Their treaty table is in **Stage 5** under a 2018 rights-recognition MOU ([BC Treaty Commission](https://bctreaty.ca/ktunaxa-nation/)).

On terminology: use **Secwépemc** for the people and nation. "Shuswap" is an exonym that survives only in proper names such as Shuswap Band and Columbia Shuswap Regional District ([Wikipedia: Secwépemc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secw%C3%A9pemc)). Write "Ktunaxa and Secwépemc **peoples**" and "Métis" with the accent ([Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/blog/ktunaxa-nation)). Treat the Shuswap Band as Secwépemc, not as part of the Ktunaxa Nation Council. An older provincial guide lists Golden as "Ktunaxa, Okanagan and Shuswap." No current local acknowledgement includes Okanagan, so do not use it ([CNC guide](https://cnc.bc.ca/docs/default-source/default-document-library/indigenous-territory-guide.pdf?sfvrsn=60123e83_0)). The current Round 3 prompt has a specific problem. It says "whose territory **this was** long before Golden existed", in the past tense. Every local acknowledgement describes the territory as unceded now, and the text should say so.

BC offers well-documented partnership models. BC has **62 Community Forest Agreements, 33 of which are held by or in partnership with First Nations** ([BCCFA](https://www.bccfa.ca/community-forestry)). **Cheakamus** near Whistler is a three-way equal partnership of the Squamish Nation, Lil'wat Nation and Resort Municipality of Whistler, with "equal standing for all three groups" on its board ([Cheakamus](https://cheakamuscommunityforest.com/about/)). **Williams Lake** is a 50/50 limited partnership between the City and Williams Lake First Nation (T'exelcemc). Its first payouts in 2018 were **$987,000 to the City and $861,000 to WLFN**, including repayment of start-up loans ([Williams Lake Tribune](https://wltribune.com/news/williams-lake-community-forest-shareholders-reap-first-profits)). It has also run cultural and prescribed burns ([Williams Lake Tribune](https://www.wltribune.com/local-news/bringing-back-indigenous-practices-in-bc-wildfire-management-8034115)). **Burns Lake** paid **$1,556,074 to each of three partners**: the Village, the Wet'suwet'en First Nation and Ts'il Kaz Koh First Nation ([Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-comfor-reports-strong-fiscal-year-6309850)). The research found **no FNESC rule on students role-playing a specific Nation**. The First Peoples Principles of Learning do say that "some knowledge is sacred and only shared with permission" ([FNESC](https://www.fnesc.ca/first-peoples-principles-of-learning/)). The advice below is therefore an inference: students should not play the Shuswap Band, the Ktunaxa or the Métis, and SD6 Indigenous Education should review the round before it runs.

## Golden has a veneer and LVL mill, not a sawmill, and carbon is a long shot

**Golden's sawmill closed in the early 1970s** when the site moved into plywood. The first laminated veneer lumber (LVL) line opened in 1997, mainly using Douglas-fir, and a second followed in 1999 ([Kootenay Business](https://kootenaybiz.com/golden/article/a_main_player_in_golden)). Evans Forest Products sold the Golden plants to Louisiana-Pacific in 1999 for **$130 million** ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/forest-company-takeover-1.187318)). LP sold them to **Pacific Woodtech on August 1, 2022** ([LP](https://investor.lpcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lp-building-solutions-completes-sale-engineered-wood-products/)). At the transfer the operation employed **about 435 people locally, plus about 200 through contractors** ([BC Gov transfer notice](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/transfers/lp_to_pwt_transfer_info.pdf)). A later report put it at **about 350 plus about 200**. That report also noted plywood production had stopped "for the time being" and that the mill was still hiring ([Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/local-business/pacific-woodtech-owners-golden-bc/)). The game's "Golden's sawmill says it will close" is wrong, and its epilogue line "Its site is condos now" describes a real, operating employer. Both should be recast as clearly hypothetical.

Carbon offsets have worked for exactly one BC community forest at scale. **Cheakamus** cut its harvest from **41,170 m³ to 21,000 m³ a year** under ecosystem-based management ([Cheakamus](https://cheakamuscommunityforest.com/ecosystem-based-management-plan/)). It sells **10,000–15,000 tCO₂e a year for about $100,000** ([Ecotrust Canada](https://ecotrust.ca/priorities/climate/cheakamus-community-forest-carbon-offsets/)). Under BC's output-based pricing system its sales rose **six-fold in 2025**, including a single **$600,000+** purchase by Newmont ([Business in Vancouver](https://www.biv.com/news/resources-agriculture/new-carbon-pricing-fuels-600-revenue-surge-for-bc-community-forest-12259045)). One number does not fit: $100,000 for 10,000–15,000 t implies about $7–10/t, while the same coverage cites about $30/t on the voluntary market. The two figures probably come from different years. Only Cheakamus and a small Quadra Island project have sold credits in the government market. A BCCFA manager called the route **"such a dead end for us."** Barriers include the provincial requirement that community forests keep a steady harvest ([BIV](https://www.biv.com/news/environment/experts-warn-bcs-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-12291061)). No real case was found of a carbon contract blocking fuel treatment. The game's tension between thinning and carbon is plausible design, not a documented event.

## Recommended config values, each tied to its anchor

Every dollar value already in `content/config.js` falls inside a real-world range. The recommendation is therefore to **keep the numbers, change the labels and prompts, and tell the room where each number comes from**. That also keeps the current tuning. `node tools/simulate.mjs` currently reports no tuning failures, and holding the figures preserves that. ## Conclusion

The research does not overturn the game's economics. It gives them provenance. The placeholder figures turn out to be about the right size for a 20,000 m³ community forest, and that forest nearly existed in Golden. So the facilitator can present the numbers as "based on Nakusp, sized to Golden's lost allocation" without any retuning. The factual risks are in the words. A sawmill that closed half a century ago, a real employer described as condos, and a past-tense description of unceded territory are the lines a sharp student or teacher would catch.

The real history is also a better teaching story than the game's invented one. A community was promised a forest, lost it to a provincial reallocation, and has now rebuilt the effort as a partnership led under a First Nation's name. That is exactly the sequence the game asks students to reason through: value capture, then governance, then whose forest it is. The debrief should tell it plainly. The two biggest remaining unknowns are a community forest's real fixed operating costs and the current status of Kenpesq't. Both are one phone call away: to NACFOR's manager and to the Golden & Area Community Forest Team.
