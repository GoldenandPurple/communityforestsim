# Markets, Mills and Policy: How BC Community Forests and Forestry Towns Weathered Shocks (2015–2026)

Method note: This was compiled 2026-11-05 from web search results. Direct fetches of bccfa.ca failed because the domain is blocked in this session's network settings, so the BCCFA items below rely on search snippets and archive.bccfa.ca reposts. Confidence: **High** = official or primary source, or several outlets agree. **Medium** = one reputable outlet or a search snippet. **Low** = aggregator or unverified.

## Q1. Lumber price swings, US softwood duties, and community forest revenues

### Takeaway
Lumber hit record highs in spring 2021, about US$1,600–1,670 per thousand board feet. Some community forests paid their largest dividends ever in 2020–2022. After that, low prices plus fast-rising US duties squeezed margins: the combined duty went from 8.05% to 14.54% in Aug 2024 and to about 35% in Aug 2025, and a 10% Section 232 tariff was added on top in Oct 2025. Community forests saw lean years, and some found their dividend levies "unrealistic."

### Cited Findings
**Price boom (2020–2021)**
- BC-milled 2x4 lumber reached a record of just over US$1,600 per thousand board feet at the end of April 2021. Stumpage rose much less than prices: the provincial average was $20.59/m³ in Jan–Apr 2020 and $29.66/m³ in Jan–Apr 2021. — [Focus on Victoria](https://www.focusonvictoria.ca/forests/67/) (Medium)
- Central 1 Economics (Feb 2023) puts the peak at US$1,670 per thousand board feet. It projected forestry GDP would fall 5–10% in 2023. — [Central 1 Economic Commentary, Feb 2023](https://www.central1.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ECON_COMMENTARY_feb-2023_forestry.pdf) (High)

**Record community forest dividends**
- Cheam/Chilliwack-area example (CLCCF, Fraser Valley): each partner received $150,000 at the end of 2021, the largest dividend in the forest's history. The manager said, "With lumber prices being exceptionally strong we were in the position to issue significant dividend cheques to all of the partners." — [Fraser Valley News Network](https://fraservalleynewsnetwork.com/?p=66407); see also [Hope Standard: District of Hope receives $150,000](https://www.hopestandard.com/news/district-of-hope-receives-150000-from-community-forest-project/) (Medium; the CLCCF acronym comes from a search snippet)
- Powell River Community Forest paid a record dividend of more than $2.8M in May 2020. For 2022 it declared a new record of $3,825,934 at its April 2023 AGM. — [BCCFA: Powell River windfall profits](https://bccfa.ca/powell-river-community-forest-has-year-of-windfall-profits); [archive.bccfa.ca](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=3733) (Medium-High)
- Sunshine Coast Community Forest (Sechelt) had a "record year for revenue." — [Coast Reporter](https://coastreporter.net/local-news/record-year-for-community-forest-revenue-3411504) (Medium; exact year not confirmed from the snippet)
- West Boundary Community Forest projected about $400,000 in dividends for 2021, describing it as a "conservative estimate." This was a forecast, not a result. — [Boundary Creek Times](https://www.boundarycreektimes.com/news/community-forest-dividends-on-track-to-hit-400000-next-year) (Medium)
- Wells Gray Community Forest (Clearwater) said it had put $3M back into the community over 10 years (reported around 2019–2020). — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5480900) (Medium)

**Lean years**
- Kaslo Community Forest 2024 AGM minutes: a $2/m³ levy on all wood sold, meant for the Community Dividend account, "has proved to be unrealistic in times of low log prices." Members voted on how the dividend, "if any," should be paid out. — [Kaslo CF 2024 AGM Minutes](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2024-AGM-Minutes.pdf) (High, primary)
- Precedent: Sunshine Coast Community Forest posted a loss in 2019 and paid no extraordinary dividend that year, though it still paid its regular dividend to the District of Sechelt. — [Coast Reporter](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/community-forest-posts-loss-hopes-to-resume-logging-in-fall-3415944) (Medium)
- Global Wood, Jan 2025: the Canadian sector was hit by "a one-two punch of low lumber prices, and American tariffs and duties." It counted 21 permanent or indefinite mill closures since 2023. — [Global Wood](https://globalwood.org/news/2026/news_20260130c.htm) (Medium; the URL suggests Jan 2026, so the date is uncertain)

**US softwood lumber duties**
- Aug 13, 2024: the 5th administrative review set the combined AD/CVD rate for most exporters at 14.54%, up from 8.05%. It took effect around Aug 19, 2024. — [Global Affairs Canada statement, Aug 2024](https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2024/08/statement-by-minister-ng-on-us-department-of-commerce-fifth-review-of-duties-on-canadian-softwood-lumber.html); [CBC: US nearly doubles duty](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7294054) (High)
- 2025: the anti-dumping rate rose to 20.56% (effective July 29, 2025). Countervailing duties more than doubled (effective Aug 12, 2025). The combined rate is about 35.19% for most firms. Canfor's company-specific rate is 47.65% (35.53% AD + 12.12% CVD). — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11305057/softwood-lumber-tariffs-us-anti-dumping); [CKPG Today](https://ckpgtoday.ca/2025/07/25/u-s-imposing-20-56-anti-dumping-duties-on-canadian-softwood/) (High)
- A separate 10% Section 232 "national security" tariff applied from Oct 14, 2025, on top of AD/CVD. — [Global Affairs Canada, softwood lumber](https://www.international.gc.ca/controls-controles/softwood-bois_oeuvre/index.aspx?view=d&lang=eng) (High)
- Apr 9, 2026: Commerce announced preliminary results of the 7th administrative reviews. — [GAC recent developments](https://www.international.gc.ca/controls-controles/softwood-bois_oeuvre/recent.aspx?lang=eng) (High that this happened; the rates were not retrieved)
- About C$14 billion in softwood duties was on deposit (date not confirmed). — [Treefrog](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=360260) (Low-Medium)

### Inferences
- A simulation "event card" sequence could run: price boom (2021) → record dividend → price slump plus duty hike (2023–24) → duties at about 35% plus a 10% tariff (2025). This sequence is well supported.
- Community forests sell logs, not lumber. That means duties and lumber prices hit them indirectly, through lower log prices and fewer buyers. The Kaslo levy comment fits this.

### Gaps
- No province-wide figure for total community forest dividends by year. BCCFA indicators reports would have it, but bccfa.ca was blocked.
- Lumber price averages for 2023–2025 were not retrieved.
- Final 7th-review rates (2026) were not found.

## Q2. Interior BC mill closures and curtailments, 2019–2026, and community forest responses

### Takeaway
Since 2019 there has been a near-continuous wave of Interior closures, driven by shrinking timber supply after the mountain pine beetle and wildfires, high log costs, low prices and duties. Single closures typically hit 150–300 direct jobs in towns of a few thousand people. COFI estimates about 10,000 direct, indirect and induced jobs were lost in 2023 alone. Documented community forest responses include receiving transition money (Wells Gray), being proposed as a home for released wood (Dungate/Houston), and later provincial expansions of community forests in hard-hit areas (Vanderhoof, Fort St. James, Fraser Lake, Mackenzie).

### Cited Findings
**2019 wave**
- By Aug 2019, an estimated 3,984 workers at 22 BC mills had been hit by closures and curtailments since Jan 1, 2019. — [Wood Business](https://www.woodbusiness.ca/heres-how-many-bc-sawmills-have-taken-downtime-so-far/) (Medium-High)
- **Vavenby (Canfor):** permanent closure announced June/July 2019, more than 170 jobs (the mayor of Clearwater said at least 172 direct jobs). Canfor's tenure was sold to Interfor. Under the deal, Canfor put $500,000 into the Wells Gray Community Forest, plus a $200,000 legacy fund with the District of Clearwater. The Simpcw First Nation and the District opposed the transfer. — [CBC: Some forestry jobs returning after transfer](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5480900); [CFJC: Simpcw opposition](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=763571); [CBC: Mayor says bureaucracy delaying progress](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5470945) (High)
- **Fort St. James (Conifex):** curtailments in Feb (3 weeks) and May (4 weeks) 2019. The mill was then sold for $39M to a buyer planning a rebuild, and the town declared a local state of financial crisis. — [CBC: Conifex to sell Fort St. James sawmill](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5188825) (High)
- **Mackenzie (2019):** Conifex curtailed for weeks. The Parallel 55 finger-joint mill shut because local sawmills could not supply trim ends, which shows how one closure cascades into others. More than 20% of Mackenzie's workforce was directly or indirectly affected, and a large rally was held in Aug 2019. — [CFJC: Big rally in Mackenzie](https://cfjctoday.com/2019/08/23/big-rally-in-mackenzie-b-c-draws-attention-to-continuing-lumber-crisis/); [CFJC: shutdowns add to job losses](https://cfjctoday.com/2019/06/14/shutdown-cuts-at-b-c-pulp-and-sawmills-add-to-forestry-job-losses/) (Medium-High)

**2023**
- **Canfor, Jan 2023:** permanent closure of the Chetwynd sawmill and pellet plant (about 157 jobs) and closure of the Houston sawmill for "redevelopment" (about 300 workers). CBC's headline was "affecting 490 jobs." — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6726399); [Pique](https://www.piquenewsmagazine.com/bc-news/chetwynd-mills-closing-permanently-6436870) (High)
- Of the 440 Chetwynd and Houston employees affected, 80 took jobs elsewhere in Canfor and 70 were retiring or applying for bridging-to-retirement support. — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/canfor-job-losses-1.6803157) (Medium-High)
- Canfor promised a new $200M Houston mill in Sept 2023, then suspended the plan, and later said it would not replace the mill. — [CBC: Canfor promises $200M mill](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6967365); [Black Press: Canfor cancels plans](https://bpbpd.wpengine.com/news/canfor-cancels-plans-to-replace-its-houston-sawmill) (High; exact cancellation date not captured)
- **Community forest angle (Houston):** MLA John Rustad suggested the District-owned Dungate Community Forest could take on more wood if Canfor was not using it. — [Haida Gwaii Observer](https://www.haidagwaiiobserver.com/news/mla-calls-for-strong-response-following-houston-mill-closure-announcement/) (Medium; no confirmation it happened)
- Central 1 estimated 850 jobs lost from sawmill and pulp closures announced in just early 2023. — [The Tyee](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2024/10/30/Inside-Fight-Save-Forestry-Jobs/) (Medium)
- COFI: about 10,000 jobs lost in 2023, half direct and half indirect or induced. The BC harvest fell from 50M m³ (2021) to 43M (2022) to 35M (2023), against a typical cut of about 60M. — [KelownaNow](https://amp.kelownanow.com/watercooler/news/news/BC_Interior/There_s_a_lot_at_stake_BC_s_forestry_industry_lost_10_000_jobs_in_2023) (Medium-High)
- BC forestry employment was about 79,000 in 2001 and about 44,000 in 2023. — [The Tyee](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2024/10/30/Inside-Fight-Save-Forestry-Jobs/) (Medium)

**2024–2025**
- **Fraser Lake (West Fraser):** permanent closure announced Jan 2024. About 175 jobs (140 USW members) in a village of about 965 people. The company cited no access to "economically viable fibre." — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7091613); [MyPGNow](https://www.myprincegeorgenow.com/190572/featured/west-fraser-permanently-closes-fraser-lake-sawmill) (High)
- **Canfor, late 2024:** closed its Vanderhoof (Plateau) and Fort St. John mills, about 500 jobs. — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7313849) (High)
- Canfor's Northwood pulp line in Prince George was also curtailed in 2024. — [Radio NL](https://www.radionl.com/?p=135811) (Medium)
- COFI, May 2024: "escalating closures and curtailments" were a growing concern. — [PaperAge](https://www.paperage.com/2024news/05-13-2024cofi-statement-on-mill-closures-curtailments.html) (High)
- **100 Mile House (West Fraser):** closure announced Nov 2025, about 165 jobs. The mayor estimated at least 500 more indirect jobs would be affected. The company cited timber access, weak demand, higher duties and tariffs. — [CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/2025/11/08/100-mile-house-mayor-profoundly-disappointed-by-impending-sawmill-closure/) (High)

**Later policy response in closure-hit regions**
- Sept 2025: the province expanded the Vanderhoof, Fort St. James and Fraser Lake community forests in the Prince George TSA. — [Treefrog](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=354291); [BC Gov 2025FOR0041](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025FOR0041-000920) (Medium-High)
- 2026: 20,000 m³ allocated to the McLeod Lake Mackenzie Community Forest. — [BC Gov 2026FOR0047](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0047-001063) (Medium-High)

### Inferences
- The pattern "the mill closes, but the community forest's tenure stays with the town" is the core lesson. Tenure transfers like Vavenby to Interfor moved cutting rights out of the community, while community forest tenure cannot be sold away.
- A closure removes a local log buyer. Community forests then face longer hauls to distant mills and lower net log prices, which is a good simulation mechanic. This is inferred, not documented for a specific forest here.

### Gaps
- Tolko closures were not found in my searches (e.g., Kelowna 2020; Lavington 2025 was reportedly announced).
- I found no concrete documented case of a named community forest pivoting log sales after a specific closure, such as Dungate after Houston. Interfor-specific BC curtailments were not retrieved.

## Q3. Community forests and community-owned enterprises keeping value local

### Takeaway
The best-documented examples are Harrop-Procter's small FSC-certified value-added mill and Revelstoke's city-owned community forest, which spun off a district energy utility burning sawmill residue. BCCFA indicators consistently show more jobs per cubic metre than industry.

### Cited Findings
- **Harrop-Procter Community Co-operative:** holds an 11,000 ha community forest under a 25-year renewable agreement. Its subsidiary, Harrop-Procter Forest Products, makes FSC-certified decking, siding, paneling, flooring, timbers and cedar lumber. It employs 5 full-time staff plus part-time forester, office and bookkeeping roles. It produces about 7 times the provincial average jobs per m³. It won the Robin Hood Memorial Award (2017). — [BC Gov story: Future bright for Kootenay sawmill](https://news.gov.bc.ca/stories/future-bright-for-growth-at-kootenay-sawmill); [Wood Business](https://www.woodbusiness.ca/sawmill-provides-environmental-and-economic-benefits-for-community-forest-in-bc-4777/); [Kootenay Biz](https://kootenaybiz.com/forestry/article/community_control_exemplifies_small_scale_forestry); [BCCFA archive](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=3781) (Medium-High; some details dated)
- 2021 provincial funding helped a community forest "get more value from less" (Robson Valley/McBride). — [Rocky Mountain Goat, Mar 2021](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2021/03/funding-will-help-community-forest-get-more-value-from-less/) (Medium)
- **Revelstoke:** the City became sole shareholder of the community forest corporation (Revelstoke Community Forest Corp.) in the early 1990s. That success led to the city-owned Revelstoke Community Energy Corporation in 1997. Its plant has run since 2005 on sawmill residue next to Downie Timber's dry kilns, supplying steam to the kilns and hot water to about 8–10 buildings, including the Community Centre. One official cited about 4,000 t of residue per year producing about 10,000 MWh. Fires later put the plant out of service for months, and a Bio-Heat Summit looked at fixing the aging system. — [FVB Energy](https://fvbenergy.com/astra-portfolio/revelstoke/); [Waterbucket case study](https://waterbucket.ca/gi/2009/09/28/integrated-resource-recovery-case-study-revelstoke-community-energy-system/); [Revelstoke Mountaineer](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/revelstoke-hosts-bio-heat-summit/) (Medium; building counts conflict, 8 vs 10)
- **Jobs intensity:** BCCFA indicators found community forests create about 50% more jobs per m³ than the industry average (2015), 63% more (2018, 40 forests surveyed, 93% of operating members), and more than twice as many (2021). The 2015 average AAC was 30,400 m³. — [Rocky Mountain Goat 2015](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2015/03/community-forests-create-50-more-jobs-than-industry-average-report/); [Wood Business 2018](https://www.woodbusiness.ca/bc-community-forest-association-releases-2018-indicators-report-5156/); [BCCFA 2021 press release](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2021-BCCFA-Press-Release-Indicators-Report.pdf) (Medium-High)
- **Carbon:** Cheakamus Community Forest (Whistler) sold $600,000 of carbon offsets in a single sale to a mining company. It is one of only a handful of successful BC forest carbon projects. — [Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/BC/615307/Experts-warn-B-C-s-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-) (Medium)

### Inferences
- Value-added work and energy are hedges. A small mill or heat plant keeps some local employment and revenue when the big mill closes, but these are small (5–10 jobs), not replacements for a 300-job mill.

### Gaps
- Burns Lake / Williams Lake biomass examples were not verified. My searches returned only the 2012 Babine explosion coverage.
- Timber-frame and firewood enterprise examples were not found.

## Q4. Policy shifts affecting community forests

### Takeaway
Major shifts include the 2021 old-growth deferrals (2.6M ha proposed), BC's 2021 target of 20% of the AAC held by First Nations (reported reached), a 2024–25 mandate to expand the community forest program with expansions rolling out in 2025–26, and a forest carbon offset regime that remains in draft and revision.

### Cited Findings
- **Old-growth deferrals (Nov 2021):** 2.6M ha proposed. The government estimated about 4,500 jobs at risk if deferrals became permanent. COFI estimated 18,000 jobs and about $400M per year in lost revenue, assuming a harvest drop of 10M m³ per year. The province committed $185M over 3 years for worker and community supports, and a later budget projected a $700M drop in forest revenues, partly blamed on reduced old-growth harvest. — [The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2021/11/10/Counting-Job-Cost-Halting-Old-Growth-Logging/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/8347841/a-gut-punch-forestry-sector-pushes-back-against-b-c-plan-to-defer-old-growth-logging/amp); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/opinion/les-leyne-bc-defers-cutting-big-swaths-of-old-growth-forests-thousands-of-jobs-at-risk-5225906) (High)
- **Premier on "exhausted forests" (Jan 2023):** context for AAC decline after the mountain pine beetle and fires. — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6693769) (Medium)
- **First Nations tenure:** the 2021 goal of 20% of the AAC held by First Nations is reported as reached. Lake Babine Nation's tenure was increased by more than 2,000% (July 2025) after a collaborative agreement with BC Timber Sales. — [BC Gov 2025FOR0032](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025FOR0032-000714) (High)
- **Community forest expansion mandate:** the Forests Minister's mandate letter (reported Jan 2025) directs expansion of the community forest program, a BC NDP election promise. — [BCCFA](https://www.bccfa.ca/post/community-forest-expansion-confirmed-in-ministry-of-forests-mandated-letter) (Medium)
- Northern communities called for community forest expansion. — [BCCFA archive](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=14412) (Medium)
- **Expansions:** Babine Lake Community Forest (Granisle and Lake Babine Nation) requested and received an increase. Vanderhoof, Fort St. James and Fraser Lake community forests were expanded (Sept 2025). Cortes Island was invited to apply (Nov 2025). McLeod Lake Mackenzie got 20,000 m³ and North Island Community Forest more than 8,100 m³ (2026). First Nations Woodland tenure availability rose from 42,650 to 162,500 m³, with 77,000 m³ for new community forests (date unconfirmed). — [BC Gov 2026FOR0047](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0047-001063); [Treefrog](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=354291); [BCCFA expansion PDF](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Community-Forest-Program-Expansion.pdf) (Medium-High; the PDF could not be fetched)
- **BC Timber Sales reforms** were part of the provincial response to the forestry crisis. — [PentictonNow](https://www.pentictonnow.com/watercooler/news/news/Provincial/A_renewed_relationship_BC_responds_to_forestry_crisis_with_BC_Timber_Sales_reforms) (Low-Medium; date not captured)
- **Carbon offsets:** BC's Forest Carbon Offset Protocol 1.0 (2011) was repealed in 2015. FCOP 2.0 drafts were released in 2021 and 2023 after stakeholder pushback, and the protocol is still in revision. Experts warn BC's forest carbon market is hitting a "dead end." — [BC offset protocols](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/climate-change/industry/offset-projects/offset-protocols); [FCOP 2.0 draft 2023](https://gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/climate-change/offsets/offsets-portfolio/fcop_20_draft_2023.pdf); [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/192323/?site=nbp); [Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/BC/615307/Experts-warn-B-C-s-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-) (Medium-High)

### Inferences
- In the simulation, "policy" events can open opportunities (an AAC expansion offer, a partnership with a First Nation) as well as closing them (deferrals, uncertain carbon rules).

### Gaps
- "Bill 23 / forestry revitalization" could not be verified. I found no source tying a Bill 23 to forestry, so it should not be used without checking.
- No claim that BC paused carbon offsets in 2023 could be verified. Sources show only drafts and revision.
- Specific AAC reduction figures for named TSAs after the beetle were not retrieved.

## Q5. Socioeconomic facts for the classroom

### Takeaway
BC has about 62 community forests (2026 government figure). They create about 1.5–2 times more jobs per m³ than industry. Forestry employment has roughly halved since 2001, and many Interior towns of 1,000–5,000 people depend on one mill.

### Cited Findings
- 62 community forests throughout BC (2026). — [BC Gov 2026FOR0047](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0047-001063) (High)
- The average community forest AAC was about 30,400 m³ (2015). — [BCCFA archive](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=2556) (Medium)
- About 44,000 BC forestry jobs in 2023, down from about 79,000 in 2001. — [The Tyee](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2024/10/30/Inside-Fight-Save-Forestry-Jobs/) (Medium)
- Town-scale impacts: Fraser Lake lost about 175 jobs in a village of about 965 people (2024) [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7091613). In Mackenzie (2019), more than 20% of the workforce was affected [CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/2019/08/23/big-rally-in-mackenzie-b-c-draws-attention-to-continuing-lumber-crisis/). In 100 Mile House, 165 direct jobs plus about 500 indirect [CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/2025/11/08/100-mile-house-mayor-profoundly-disappointed-by-impending-sawmill-closure/). (High)
- "It just guts a small community": a CBC personal-impact story. — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6727838) (High)
- Mill closures threaten to "punch holes in the fabric of rural BC towns." — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6741058); [BIV: Small-town BC hit hard](https://biv.com/article/2023/02/small-town-bc-hit-hard-forestry-downturn) (High)

### Inferences
- A fair classroom framing: "In a town of about 1,000, one mill closure can remove 1 in 5 jobs," supported by the Fraser Lake and Mackenzie examples.

### Gaps
- Population served by community forests (BCCFA has cited figures, but I could not retrieve them).
- The Interior's share of BC forestry jobs.
- An official count of single-mill-dependent towns (the "140 communities" figure was not verified).
