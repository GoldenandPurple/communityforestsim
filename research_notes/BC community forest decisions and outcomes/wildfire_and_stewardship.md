# Wildfire, Fuel Management and Stewardship Decisions by BC Community Forests (2000–2026)

Method note (Nov 2026): Direct page fetches of bccfa.ca and www2.gov.bc.ca were blocked in this research session (DNS not allowed), so most findings come from search-result snippets of the cited URLs. Confidence is marked as **High** (multiple consistent sources or a government release), **Medium** (single snippet, plausible, dated), or **Low** (undated, ambiguous, or inferred). The full FPInnovations Logan Lake report and the BCCFA "Enhancing Wildfire Resilience" report (2024) were not read in full; a writer wanting quantitative fire-behaviour numbers should consult them directly.

## 1. WUI fuel treatments and whether they helped during a real fire

### Takeaway
Logan Lake (2021 Tremont Creek fire) is the best-documented BC case where years of community-forest and municipal fuel treatment plus FireSmart work coincided with a town surviving a large wildfire with no structures lost; a BC Wildfire Service / FPInnovations case study (May 2023) examined it. Most other community forests (Williams Lake, Kaslo, Harrop-Procter, Lower North Thompson, Wells Gray, Esk'etemc) have documented treatments (hectares, dollars) but no documented "treatment stopped a fire" outcome yet.

### Cited Findings

**Logan Lake Community Forest / District of Logan Lake — 2021 Tremont Creek fire (High confidence on outcome; Medium on mechanism)**
- Tremont Creek fire threatened Logan Lake on **August 14, 2021**, approaching from the north; crews reinforced fuel treatment areas on three sides of the town with aerial retardant and later a burnout using the treated areas to reinforce a control line. — [FPInnovations/BCWS case study PDF (gov.bc.ca)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/public-safety-and-emergency-services/wildfire-status/prevention/fire-fuel-management/fuels-management/wildfire-fuel_treatment_encounters_-_assessing_fuel_treatment_effectiveness_logan_lake_bc_20230403.pdf); [FPInnovations library record (Hvenegaard & Baxter, May 2023)](https://library.fpinnovations.ca/link/fpipub10535)
- Fire burned **63,548 ha** before containment; Logan Lake (pop. ~2,000, 60 km SW of Kamloops) lost **no structures**. — [ICLR Cat Tales Jul/Aug 2021](https://issuu.com/iclr/docs/cat_tales_julyaug_2021/7); [Castanet Kamloops](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Kamloops/476542/Kamloops-filmmaker-releasing-doc-on-Logan-Lake-s-FireSmart-success-in-face-of-2021-Tremont-Creek-fire)
- The fire chief reported untreated forest burned over, but fire behaviour became much less active on reaching treated ground; the BC government fuel-management literature page says high-intensity spread was moderated in fuel-reduced stands. (Qualitative; no rate-of-spread numbers found.) — [Radio NL, Aug 31 2021](https://www.radionl.com/2021/08/31/logan-lake-fire-chief-not-letting-his-guard-down-even-as-tremont-creek-fire-is-being-held/); [BC fuel management references](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/wildfire-status/prevention/fire-fuel-management/fuel-management/references-and-literature)
- Mayor credited ~18 years of FireSmart work; also credited ~$45 residential sprinklers with buying "that little tiny bit of extra time." — [Business in Vancouver](https://www.biv.com/news/environment/45-sprinklers-helped-save-bc-town-wildfire-says-mayor-8268348); [The Logan Lake FireSmart success story](https://www.wildfireawareness.org/post/the-logan-lake-firesmart-success-story)
- The community forest had planned a fuel treatment project for summer 2021, but the Tremont fire moved into the proposed treatment blocks before operations began (a "too late" counterexample for the simulation). — [search snippet from Logan Lake/FESBC coverage, CFJC Sep 23 2021](https://cfjctoday.com/2021/09/23/logan-lake-hoping-to-step-up-fire-mitigation-efforts-with-funding-from-forest-enhancement-society-of-bc/) (Medium)
- History: the District began removing beetle-killed pine in **2004**, treating **180 ha** of dead standing lodgepole pine. — [LLCF Wildfire Risk Reduction page](https://www.llcf.ca/wildfire-risk-reduction) (Medium)
- **2013**: since 2005 the District and Wildfire Management Branch had completed **21 fuel treatment projects on 87.4 ha** of Crown land (~134 ha counting overlapping treatment types); Logan Lake recognized as a FireSmart community. — [BC Gov News 2013FLNR0228-001765](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2013FLNR0228-001765) (High)
- **2019**: Logan Lake Community Forest won a provincial community forest award; as of 2018 it had a 10-year landscape-level wildfire risk plan for its whole tenure and reforested **400+ ha in 2018**. — [BC Gov News 2019FLNR0169-001239](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2019FLNR0169-001239) (High)
- Post-fire FESBC-supported projects: Critical Infrastructure Resilience prescription (**115 ha**), Logan Lake East Fuel Hazard Reduction prescription (**65 ha**), Post-Harvest Fuel Treatments (**108 ha** operational). — [CFJC, Sep 23 2021](https://cfjctoday.com/2021/09/23/logan-lake-hoping-to-step-up-fire-mitigation-efforts-with-funding-from-forest-enhancement-society-of-bc/) (Medium; year of each project not confirmed)
- Later Logan Lake innovations: goat grazing for wildfire risk reduction (ICLR, 2025); Perimeter Solutions fire-retardant donation (July 2024). — [ICLR Logan Lake goat grazing PDF](https://www.iclr.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/16_LOGAN-LAKE.pdf); [AerialFire, Jul 11 2024](https://aerialfiremag.com/2024/07/11/perimeter-solutions-fire-retardant-donation-bolsters-logan-lakes-wildfire-defense/)
- Documentary "The Test" about Logan Lake's wildfire resilience. — [TreeFrog Creative](https://treefrogcreative.ca/the-test-a-documentary-about-community-wildfire-resilience-in-the-town-of-logan-lake-bc)
- Aug 2026: Logan Lake mayor urged vigilance as the Guichon Creek fire risk subsided (another fire-season test; details not reviewed). — [CFJC, Aug 4 2026](https://cfjctoday.com/2026/08/04/logan-lake-mayor-urges-vigilance-as-guichon-creek-fire-risk-subsides/) (Low on details)

### Inferences
- For the simulation, Logan Lake gives a defensible "investment paid off" storyline: early beetle-pine removal (2004), cumulative small treatments (87–134 ha by 2013), whole-tenure fire plan (2018), then a 63,548 ha fire in 2021 with zero structures lost. But the outcome was multi-causal (FireSmart, sprinklers, suppression, weather), so a simulation should model treatments as lowering damage probability, not guaranteeing safety.
- Typical community forest WUI treatments are small (10–300 ha per project) and cost roughly $90k–$560k per FESBC grant; per-hectare cost implied by Kaslo (≈$9,700/ha for 10 ha, 2025) is my arithmetic and reflects expensive hand work near homes.
- Logan Lake's planned-2021 project overtaken by fire, and Clearwater's "got lucky," are good cautionary events (delay risk; weather randomness).

### Gaps
- No quantitative fire-behaviour data (rate of spread, flame length) from the Logan Lake report — PDF could not be fetched.
- No documented case found where a Williams Lake, Kaslo, Harrop-Procter, Lower North Thompson or Esk'etemc treatment was later tested by a wildfire.
- Williams Lake Esler fuel break and $561,278 grant years not confirmed from snippets.

## 2. Prescribed and cultural burning led by or partnered with community forests / First Nations

### Takeaway
The Williams Lake Community Forest (co-owned by Williams Lake First Nation and the City) and the Esk'etemc Community Forest (via Alkali Resource Management) have run repeated prescribed burns with BC Wildfire Service since about 2018–2022, typically 30–100 ha per burn; Lytton First Nation and Skeetchestn provide strong First-Nation-led fire stories, though outcome data are mostly qualitative.

### Cited Findings
- **Williams Lake Community Forest**: managed through a partnership of Williams Lake First Nation and the City of Williams Lake; a **2024** burn (~**40 ha**) continued an earlier spacing treatment to reduce stand density; a **2025** burn of **29 ha** targeted juniper density. — [BCWS: Prescribed burn planned for Williams Lake Community Forest](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/prescribed-burn-planned-for-williams-lake-community-forest-2/); [BCWS ?p=19033](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/?p=19033) (Medium–High)
- **Flat Rock prescribed burn** (WLFN + WLCF) began in **2022**, with its fourth and final burn planned for **2025**. — [BCWS blog search results](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/?p=27056) (Medium)
- **WLFN cultural burns** on IR 1 with BCWS support; a **100 ha** cultural burn planned for **April 2025**, objectives: reduce wildfire risk and restore forest health. — [BCWS: Cultural burn planned within Williams Lake First Nation](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/cultural-burn-planned-within-williams-lake-first-nation-2/) (High)
- Williams Lake Community Forest also removed Douglas-fir-beetle-affected timber in Flat Rock (~200 truckloads in one winter) alongside wildfire mitigation — showing salvage + fuel reduction + burning sequenced on one block. — [BCCFA: WLCF focuses on fir-beetle, wildfire mitigation and education](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=4580) (Medium; undated)
- **Esk'etemc / ARM**: ARM (Esk'etemc-owned) works with BCWS and Cariboo Aboriginal Forestry Enterprises on controlled burns; BCWS supported a burn of up to **57 ha** in the Esk'etemc Community Forest (**September 2022**); ~**60 ha** of burns near Mayfield Lake in **2018**; the nation has an Elder-appointed Firekeeper. — [MyCariboo Now](https://www.mycariboonow.com/83735/news/prescribed-burn-is-planned-for-the-esketemc-community-forest); [Lakes District/bpbpd: Prescribed burns near Alkali](https://bpbpd.wpengine.com/news/prescribed-burns-to-take-place-near-alkali); [The Wren documentary article](https://thewrennews.ca/documentary-sparks-conversation-about-indigenous-led-wildfire-resilience); [Francis Johnson BCCFA presentation, Jun 8 2023](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Francis_Johnson_BCCFA_ResiliencyThroughFire_June82023.pdf) (Medium–High)
- **Lytton First Nation** (after the June 2021 fire destroyed most of Lytton and killed two): Green Meadows burn **24.5 ha** on Inkluckcheen IR 21 (goals: risk reduction, ecosystem restoration, LFN–BCWS cross-training); up to **39 ha** burn in Botanie Valley to boost traditional food harvests; Lot 47 prescribed fire project reduced immediate risk around Lytton. — [BCWS: Prescribed burn planned for Lytton area](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/prescribed-burn-planned-for-lytton-area/); [FireSmart BC: A Cultural Approach to Mitigating Wildfire](https://firesmartbc.ca/a-cultural-approach-to-mitigating-wildfire/) (Medium; exact years not confirmed)
- **Skeetchestn**: during the **2021** Sparks Lake fire, a community-led back-burn saved the community school and much of the town; in **2017** (Elephant Hill) the community was evacuated ~6 weeks but its own patrol team helped avert damage. — [National Academies presentation materials (SFU/Skeetchestn)](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/9fba07c8-9297-459e-a44b-246e327121af); [Reclaiming Fire and Sovereignty: The Skeetchestn's Journey](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-894bd34469ec46a591004897138985ea/Description) (Medium; not a community forest case)
- **Ts'kw'aylaxw**: co-leads northern St'át'imc post-wildfire vegetation research after the McKay Creek wildfire (2021); no results found. — [CBC Ideas: Healing the land after wildfires](https://amp.cbc.ca/radio/ideas/healing-the-land-after-wildfires-1.7125869) (Low)
- Provincial scale: **80+** cultural and prescribed burns completed between April 2025 and February 2026, many First Nation led/co-led; only Nations can determine whether a burn is "cultural." — [BCWS blog](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/?p=27056); [APTN](https://www.aptnnews.ca/?p=241641) (Medium)

### Inferences
- A realistic simulation "prescribed burn" decision: 30–100 ha per year, needs a weather window and BCWS partnership, often follows mechanical thinning/spacing on the same block (Williams Lake 2024), and can be multi-year (Flat Rock 2022–2025).
- First Nation partnership is a key enabler in the real examples (WLFN, Esk'etemc, Lytton).

### Gaps
- No measured outcome data (e.g., later fire behaviour on burned blocks) for these burns.
- No confirmed community forest tenure for Skeetchestn, Lytton, or Ts'kw'aylaxw in the sources found.

## 3. Watershed protection as a founding purpose; ecosystem-based management

### Takeaway
Harrop-Procter (pilot agreement 1999, ~11,300 ha) was born from a 1984 community watershed-protection committee and runs on an ecosystem-based plan with water as top priority; Cheakamus (Whistler) set its AAC at about half of conventional (21,000 vs 41,170 m3/yr) under EBM and funded the gap with carbon offsets; Sunshine Coast Community Forest committed to no harvest in the Chapman Creek drinking watershed for 25 years after a 2007 blockade.

### Cited Findings
- **Harrop-Procter**: residents formed the Harrop-Procter Watershed and Community Protection Committee at a public meeting on **Sept 10, 1984**; they first tried to stop all logging, then pursued a Community Forest Agreement; awarded a Community Forest Pilot Agreement in **1999** covering ~**11,300 ha** on the West Arm of Kootenay Lake. — [UBC FRST370 case](https://cases.open.ubc.ca/?p=21010); [BCCFA HP profile PDF](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/HP.pdf) (High)
- HPCF uses an ecosystem-based approach: protecting water and ecological integrity are top priorities; objectives include minimizing road and harvest impacts on hydrology and water quality; reduced logging intensity negotiated with the province; reported as BC's (then) only FSC-certified community forest. — [KootenayBiz](https://kootenaybiz.com/forestry/article/community_control_exemplifies_small_scale_forestry); [Soil Association case study](https://soilassociation.org/certification/forestry/case-studies/harrop-procter-community-forest) (Medium; FSC claim date unclear)
- Governance split: the Harrop-Procter Watershed Protection Society monitors plans; the Harrop-Procter Community Co-operative holds the licence. — [UBC wiki](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/Projects/Forest_Resource_Management_in_Harrop-Procter_Community_Forest,_BC,_Canada) (Medium)
- 2021 manager presentation lists tensions: hydrology/ECA (equivalent clearcut area) limits; reconsidering "sustained yield" and "even flow"; pace of fuel breaks. — [Leslie Resource Consulting presentation](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/leslie-resource-consulting-ppt-presentation-594774) (Low–Medium)
- **Cheakamus Community Forest**: province set AAC at **21,000 m3/yr** under the EBM plan vs **41,170 m3/yr** possible under standard management (≈49% reduction); Pique cites ~20,000 m3 working figure averaged over 5-year periods. — [BC Gov News 2015FLNR0075-000574](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015FLNR0075-000574); [Pique](https://www.piquenewsmagazine.com/whistler-news/plans-for-cheakamus-community-forest-announced-2497831) (High on figures; EBM plan year not confirmed in snippet)
- Cheakamus signed BC's first Atmospheric Benefits Sharing Agreement with a community forest (**2015**); offset project reduced emissions ~**10,000 t CO2e/yr** via avoided/modified harvest, and Ecotrust notes those practices could not be sustained without offset revenue. — [BC Gov News 2015](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015FLNR0075-000574); [Ecotrust Canada briefing](https://ecotrust.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Briefing_CheakamusCarbon.pdf) (High)
- **2021**: Cheakamus deferred old-growth logging; option floated to cut AAC to 15,000 m3; a later supply review found **13,090 m3** sustainable over 250 years (pushing harvest to younger stands). — [Pique 3881251](https://www.piquenewsmagazine.com/local-news/deferral-of-old-growth-logging-signals-paradigm-shift-for-cheakamus-community-forest-3881251); [Pique 5119140](https://piquenewsmagazine.com/local-news/old-growth-logging-at-ccf-deferred-until-end-of-year-as-board-looks-to-diversify-revenue-5119140) (Medium; 13,090 date not confirmed)
- **Sunshine Coast Community Forest / Chapman Creek**: logging resumed in **2007** after a 15-year hiatus, sparking road blockades; in **March 2008**, after shíshálh (Sechelt) representations, the community forest was removed from the Chapman and Gray watersheds; in **2013** a spacing contract in the watershed was "permanently terminated" after resident complaints. Current SCCF (2025–2029 operating plan) commits to **no harvesting in the Chapman Creek Community Watershed for 25 years**, wider-than-required stream buffers and hydrology assessments. — [Sunshine Coast Conservation Assoc.](https://thescca.ca/?p=5914); [Coast Reporter: spacing concern](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/spacing-in-chapman-creek-watershed-causes-concern-3376401); [SCCF 2025–2029 Operating Plan feedback form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSdxyFbhaxOqnJCRwMsW90jS_QQ90cJI6w_fU8K_J69nLjX7pw/viewform) (Medium–High; self-reported)
- Forest Practices Board found drinking-water protection requirements in community watersheds unclear and commitments not always enforceable; government does not monitor whether objectives are met. — [Sunshine Coast Conservation Assoc. citing FPB](https://thescca.ca/?p=49) (Medium; FPB report not directly accessed)

### Inferences
- Simulation analogues: (a) "found the forest to protect water" (Harrop-Procter), (b) "halve the cut for ecology, replace revenue with carbon" (Cheakamus), (c) "community backlash forces a no-harvest watershed commitment" (Sunshine Coast).

### Gaps
- Nelson-area watershed examples (e.g., Nelson's watershed logging for fire risk, 2021) appeared only as a headline: [Nelson Daily, Sep 2021](https://thenelsondaily.com/2021/09/logging-project-reducing-risk-wildfire-nelsons-watershed) — not confirmed as a community forest project.
- Harrop-Procter AAC figures and EBM plan date not found.

## 4. Beetle kill (MPB, spruce, Douglas-fir) and salvage decisions

### Takeaway
Community forests in the MPB zone (Burns Lake) took on extra salvage volume to capture value before decay, then planned for steep post-beetle AAC drops; Logan Lake began fuel-reduction harvesting of dead pine in 2004; Williams Lake removed Douglas-fir-beetle timber as part of fuel/fire work. Little was found on community forest spruce-beetle decisions.

### Cited Findings
- **Burns Lake Community Forest (2011)**: province approved transfer of a **5,400 ha** community salvage licence (AAC **25,000 m3**) from Lakes Timber Health & Salvage, because severe MPB damage made small-scale salvage uneconomic. — [BC Gov News 2011FOR0214-001504](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2011FOR0214-001504) (High)
- By **2021** Burns Lake CF covered **92,572 ha**, AAC **118,100 m3** green plus rights to salvage **72,951 m3/yr** of beetle-killed wood; salvage expected to end ~**2020** and AAC expected to drop to **63,000 m3** in 2020. — [Truck Logger BC, Spring 2021](https://mydigitalpublication.com/article/Burns+Lake+Community+Forest+/4030305/705073/article.html); [Burns Lake manager presentation 2018](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Burns-Lake-Frank-Varga.pdf); [Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-prepares-for-impeding-shortfall-in-timber/) (Medium; figures from different years)
- **Logan Lake**: District started removing beetle-killed pine in **2004**, **180 ha** of dead lodgepole pine (beetle salvage doubling as fuel reduction). — [LLCF](https://www.llcf.ca/wildfire-risk-reduction) (Medium)
- **Williams Lake CF**: Douglas-fir beetle timber removal in Flat Rock block, ~200 truckloads over one winter, combined with wildfire mitigation. — [BCCFA](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=4580) (Medium; undated)
- Context: spruce beetle wood stumpage cut to encourage salvage; BC Timber Sales got "no bids" on spruce-beetle wood near Mackenzie. — [Prince George Citizen](https://www.princegeorgecitizen.com/local-news/spruce-beetle-outbreak-poses-harvesting-challenges-3741176) (Medium; ~2019–2020)
- Contrast (not a community forest): Canoe Forest Products proposed salvaging beetle-killed Douglas-fir in the East Canoe Creek community watershed near Salmon Arm (Aug 2025), following a 2020 operation — illustrates watershed vs. salvage trade-off. — [TreeFrog](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=351980) (Medium)

### Inferences
- Simulation mechanic: beetle outbreak → choose fast salvage (short-term revenue spike, later AAC cliff, fuel reduction) vs. leave standing dead (habitat, but fire risk and value loss). Burns Lake's AAC trajectory (118,100 + 72,951 salvage → ~63,000 m3) is a concrete "cliff."

### Gaps
- No community forest spruce-beetle salvage decision found; no dollar values on salvage revenue.

## 5. Wildfire losses, recovery and funding sources / spending

### Takeaway
Major funders are FESBC ($79.6M in 201 community wildfire risk-reduction projects since 2016, as of 2025), the province's ~$5M BCCFA program (2021–2023/24, 45 projects, 1,362 ha), and Community Resiliency Investment/UBCM; community forests also spend their own money (~$8–10.9M). Cheslatta is the clearest example of loss: ~75% of its community forest burned in 2018, followed by a $1.25M FESBC rehabilitation grant.

### Cited Findings
- **BCCFA wildfire program**: Economic Recovery Initiative gave ~**$5 million** to BCCFA for **45** projects in **15** community forest tenures (2021–2023; one page says 2021–2024); **1,362 ha** treated. — [BCWS blog: 45 fuel mitigation projects in community forests](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/?p=15291); [BCCFA wildfire risk reduction](https://www.bccfa.ca/wildfire-risk-reduction) (High)
- **BCCFA 2024 report**: community forests treated **8,100+ ha** before 2023, investing **$8M+** of their own funds plus **$17M+** in grants; a 2024 press release says 8,000+ ha and **$9M+** own funds; an archive page says **9,000+ ha** and **$10.9M** own funds "over the past decade." — [BCCFA Enhancing Wildfire Resilience Report (Jun 2024)](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/BCCFA-Enhancing-Wildfire-Resilience-Report.pdf); [BCCFA archive](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=10590) (Medium; figures differ by time frame)
- **FESBC**: since 2016, **$79.6M** in **201** community wildfire risk-reduction projects; 64 new projects totalling **$19.8M** (March 2025); Budget 2024 added **$60M over 3 years**. All-types FESBC total: 372 projects, **$306,247,028** as of Nov 27, 2023. — [BC Gov News 2025FOR0010-000152](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025FOR0010-000152); [BC Gov News 2023FOR0069-001877](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2023FOR0069-001877) (High)
- **Cheslatta Community Forest**: 2018 wildfires burned ~**75%** of the community forest, making much of it uneconomic to harvest; FESBC gave Cheslatta Carrier Nation **$1.25M** (application Nov 2018; announced 2019) to rehabilitate severely damaged stands and use residual fibre. — [Lakes District News via bpbpd](https://bpbpd.wpengine.com/news/cheslatta-carrier-nation-undertakes-fire-rehabilitation-south-of-burns-lake); [MyPrinceGeorgeNow](https://www.myprincegeorgenow.com/125428/forest-enhancement-society-of-bc-provides-grant-to-cheslatta-carrier-nation/); [The Tyee, Jul 27 2020](https://thetyee.ca/News/2020/07/27/First-Nation-Built-Forestry-Future-From-Ashes-Past/) (High)
- **Chinook Community Forest** (Burns Lake area) salvaged burnt stands in the 2018 Shovel Lake (~92,000 ha), Verdun and Nadina (86,767 ha) fires. — [Lakes District News: Chinook open house](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/chinook-community-forest-hosts-open-house-6314521) (Medium)
- **Wells Gray CF** dividends (Community Forest Reserve) fund Clearwater's initial attack crew (up to $294,532). — [B100](https://www.b100.ca/?p=114733) (Medium)
- Example grant sizes: Kaslo $96,900 (10 ha, 2025); Harrop-Procter $460,950; Williams Lake $561,278; Esk'etemc $429,393 (3 projects); LNTCFS $373,000 (2026–27); LNTCFS $85,096 prescription (~275 ha). — sources as cited in Section 1.

### Inferences
- Simulation "funding" events can draw on realistic grant sizes ($85k–$560k) and the fact that grants often cover prescriptions (planning) separately from operational treatment.
- Community forests reinvest dividends in wildfire capacity (Wells Gray → fire crew), a good "community benefit" decision card.
