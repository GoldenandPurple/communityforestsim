# BC Community Forest Decisions: Recreation, First Nations Partnerships, and Local Conflicts (2000–2026)

Method note: About 15 web searches. Full-page fetches failed for most local news hosts (revelstokemountaineer.com, treefrogcreative.ca, coastreporter.net, therockymountaingoat.com, bpbpd.wpengine.com were blocked by the session's network settings). Most findings therefore come from **search-result snippets**, not full articles. Confidence is marked as **[High]** (several sources agree, or a primary/government source), **[Med]** (one credible source or a snippet only), or **[Low]** (snippet only, unclear date, or sources conflict). Before printing a story card, confirm any [Med]/[Low] item against the linked article.

**Important correction to the brief:** The Mount Macpherson (Revelstoke) trails dispute is mainly about **BC Timber Sales (BCTS)** cutblocks, not the Revelstoke Community Forest Corporation (RCFC). RCFC shows up in it only as one of several local licensees, and as the body residents proposed should take over the area as a community forest. If the simulation uses this as a community forest card, it should present it as "a community asks for a community forest because it lacks control," not "the community forest logged the trails."

## Recreation and trails: community forests and trail networks, agreements, harvest changes

### Takeaway
The best-documented recreation stories are (a) Wetzin'kwa (Smithers/Telkwa), where a 50+ km multi-use trail network sits inside a working community forest and harvests are planned around it, (b) Kaslo, where board updates show cutblocks designed around trails and highway views, with trails restored after harvest, and (c) Revelstoke's Mount Macpherson, where years of trail-user pressure got BCTS to add trail buffers and shrink cutblocks. The Macpherson case also shows that residents want a community forest exactly when outside tenure holders control their backyard.

### Cited Findings
**Revelstoke / Mount Macpherson (BCTS, with RCFC on the sidelines)**
- 2015–2016: A BCTS cutblock overlapping the TNT and Ridgewalk mountain-bike trails led to a rally by mountain bikers. BCTS later modified the block to keep a buffer around the TNT and Ridgewalk trails and the Main Loop Nordic ski trail. [Med] — [Revelstoke Mountaineer: mountain bikers rally](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/revelstoke-mountain-bikers-rally-against-logging/); [Revelstoke Cycling Association releases details](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/?p=12717); [Updated: Logging on Macpherson underway](https://bpbpd.wpengine.com/news/updated-logging-on-macpherson-underway)
- The City of Revelstoke and the Columbia Shuswap Regional District (CSRD) both asked the Minister of Forests for consultation and involvement. The minister responded. (The content of the response was not retrieved.) [Med] — [City to ask Minister of Forests](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/city-to-ask-minister-of-forests-to-get-involved-on-mount-macpherson-logging-issue/); [CSRD again requests consultation](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/csrd-will-again-request-logging-consultation-from-ministry/); [Forest minister responds](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/mount-macpherson-logging-forest-minister-responds-to-city-of-revelstoke-consultation-request/)

Council chose to wait and see how the minister process played out, and one councillor called it a last resort. [Med] — [Revelstoke Review: disappointment follows forestry open house](https://www.revelstokereview.com/news/disappointment-follows-revelstoke-forestry-open-house/); [Revelstoke Review: time for a plan for Mount Macpherson](https://www.revelstokereview.com/opinion/time-for-a-plan-for-mount-macpherson/)
- An online petition sought to halt logging plans for the Macpherson recreation area. [Med] — [Revelstoke Mountaineer: online petition](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/?p=1776)
- August 2025: BCTS published a Forestry Operations Map for Mount Macpherson, home to Revelstoke Nordic Ski Club and Revelstoke Cycling Association trails. Headline: "BC Timber Sales scales back logging planned for Revelstoke mountain." The Nordic ski club reportedly supports some logging for forest health. Late August 2025: cyclists were warned of about a month of logging that would "minimally" affect recreation trails. [Med] — [Boundary Creek Times / Black Press: BCTS scales back logging](https://www.boundarycreektimes.com/local-news/bc-timber-sales-scales-back-logging-planned-for-revelstoke-mountain-8185399); [TreeFrog: cyclists cautioned](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=352964)

Wildsight Revelstoke is asking the eventual licensee to minimize logging, citing cedar-hemlock stands, riparian habitat and streamflow to the Columbia. Wildsight also notes that earlier cutblocks next to trails caused windthrow that blocks routes. [Med] — [TreeFrog/Revelstoke Review: Wildsight querying industry](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=366725)

**Wetzin'kwa Community Forest (Smithers & Telkwa)**
- The tenure was established in 2007 and is held by the Town of Smithers and the Village of Telkwa through the Wetzin'kwa Community Forest Corporation. The board includes Wet'suwet'en representation. The forest covers about 32,897 ha. [Med] — [UBC FRST370 wiki: Wetzin'kwa](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/Wetzin%27kwa_Community_Forest_Corporation:_co-managed_by_Smithers_and_the_Village_of_Telkwa,_British_Columbia,_Canada)

[Med] — [Tourism Smithers summer trails guide](https://tourismsmithers.com/images/uploads/smithers-summer-trails-guide-web.pdf); [WCFC 2021–22 Annual Report](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5fc027787d0c8f249d3cbf36/t/6377eb0e38aa6e436792264a/1668803344159/2021_2022_AnnualReport_Small_PDF.pdf)
- 2024: WCFC prepared a **partial harvest** (not a clearcut) inside a predicted northern goshawk territory that also holds cross-country ski and hiking trails, with monitoring data posted to the Bulkley Valley Research Centre DataHub. [Med] — [BVRC DataHub: Wetzin'kwa](https://datahub.bvcentre.ca/group/wetzinwa-community-forest?tags=2023)
- WCFC hands out profits as community grants every year: more than $600,000 since 2009 (figure from a secondary snippet, date unclear). [Low] — [UBC FRST370 wiki](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/Wetzin%27kwa_Community_Forest_Corporation:_co-managed_by_Smithers_and_the_Village_of_Telkwa,_British_Columbia,_Canada)

**Kaslo & District Community Forest Society**
- Board updates from 2025–2026 describe harvest design for trails and views. In CP 55, heavier removal is centred upslope on old trails and roads, while areas along the power line are "reserved for visuals from the highway." Recreation clubs were notified because many trails start from the logging road system. [Med] — [KDCFS December 2025 board update](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/december-2025-general-board-meeting-update/); [KDCFS harvest preparations](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/harvest-preparations/)
- Spring 2026: CP 56 (behind the dump) was harvested in winter 2025–26. Crews are now cleaning up debris and "rehabilitating trails throughout the block." Earlier cutting permits were sold "on the stump" to Kalesnikoff/Stella Jones, with local contractors (Iron Peaks of New Denver, Sunshine Logging of Kaslo) doing the harvesting. [Med] — [KDCFS April–May 2026 board update](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/april-may-2026-general-board-meeting-update/); [KDCFS harvest preparations](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/harvest-preparations/)
- KDCFS runs public post-harvest guided tours, for example a National Forest Week tour of the 7-Mile cutting permit. [Med] — [KDCFS 7-Mile tour](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/national-forest-week-guided-post-harvest-tour-of-our-7mile-cutting-permit/)

**Valemount Community Forest**
- Community forest roads give access to recreation. In 2019 the forest's manager credited the 5-Mile recreation area with a big impact on tourism, and the forest reported being debt-free and able to invest. [Med] — [Rocky Mountain Goat 2019: community forest debt free](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2019/05/community-forest-debt-free-able-to-invest/)
- The Valemount Bike Park (VARDA, a non-profit) has 50+ miles of trails built mainly to drive tourism. A claim that the community forest gave "over $900,000" to recreation comes from an unreliable aggregator snippet. [Low] — [Singletracks](https://www.singletracks.com/mtb-trails/with-50-miles-of-trails-and-2300-feet-of-vert-valemount-bike-park-is-bcs-hidden-gem/); [ORCBC blog](https://www.orcbc.ca/blog/how-to-grow-money-from-trees)
- 2025: A change.org petition, "Hold Valemount Community Forest Accountable: Support Local Jobs and Ethical Forestry," was sent to the mayor. VCF sent a formal written response dated June 20, 2025. (Content not retrieved; the petition apparently concerned local contractor jobs.) A headline says VCF projected more than $600K profit for 2025. [Low–Med] — [change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/hold-valemount-community-forest-accountable-support-local-jobs-and-ethical-forestry); [VCF response PDF](https://www.therockymountaingoat.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025_06_20_VCF-Response-to-Mayor-re-Petition.pdf); [Ground News headline](https://ground.news/article/valemount-community-forest-predicts-over-600k-profit-for-2025)

### Inferences
- Common pattern: when the community holds the tenure (Kaslo, Wetzin'kwa), trail protection gets built into harvest design (partial cuts, visual reserves, trail rehab, notifying clubs). When an outside agency holds it (BCTS on Macpherson), residents have to petition, rally and lobby the minister, and the only result is buffers and smaller blocks. That contrast makes a strong simulation card.
- Trail users are not all on one side. The Revelstoke Nordic club reportedly supports forest-health logging while cyclists and Wildsight push back. This is a useful "stakeholders disagree" detail.

### Gaps
- I found no formal signed agreement between a community forest and a bike or trail club in the snippets. Such agreements may exist (Nelson, Cumberland, Squamish), but I could not verify any.
- Nelson, Cumberland/Comox Valley and Squamish trail stories were not researched because of the tool-call budget.
- The scale of BCTS's August 2025 reduction (hectares) and the minister's reply to Revelstoke were not retrieved.
- The content of the Valemount 2025 petition and VCF's reply is unverified.

## Visual quality / viewscape conflicts near towns and how boards responded

### Takeaway
The clearest documented community-forest conflict is the Sunshine Coast Community Forest (SCCF) on lower Mount Elphinstone (2018–2020). The board went ahead with logging after public consultation, protests and arrests followed, including a shíshálh Nation elder, and the board later went through a governance crisis. Kaslo shows the quieter alternative: highway view reserves and heavier cutting kept away from sightlines.

### Cited Findings
- About November 2018: SCCF decided to go ahead with logging a 14-ha portion of lower Mount Elphinstone despite a public rally in Sechelt on Nov. 15. Chair Glen Bonderud said: "We said we would listen to the community, and we did listen." [Med] — [Coast Reporter: logging plans unchanged, opposition kicks into gear](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/logging-plans-unchanged-opposition-kicks-into-gear-3374897); [Coast Reporter: concerns raised over SCCF cutblock](https://coastreporter.net/local-news/concerns-raised-over-sccf-cutblock-3382707); [Coast Reporter: community forest ready to log in Wilson Creek](https://coastreporter.net/local-news/community-forest-ready-to-log-in-wilson-creek-3404769)
- Protesters were arrested over SCCF logging, including 79-year-old shíshálh Nation elder Xwu'p'a'lich (Barb Higgins). [Med] — [AMMSA Raven's Eye: Sechelt elder arrested](https://ammsa.com/publications/ravens-eye/sechelt-elder-arrested-sunshine-coast-logging-protesters)
- Separate case, often confused with SCCF: **Clack Creek** (Mount Elphinstone) was a BCTS sale auctioned to Black Mount Logging in May 2019. Elphinstone Logging Focus put more than 1,000 felt hearts on trees (Oct. 19, 2019) and blockaded from Jan. 12, 2020. The company won an injunction, crews returned Feb. 4, 2020, and at least one protester was arrested. **This is not a community forest decision.** [High] — [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/weve-been-cheated-sunshine-coast-community-braces-for-logging-of-forest-at-heart-of-park-proposal/); [Maclean's](https://macleans.ca/society/environment/a-heartfelt-protest-in-b-c-s-clack-creek-forest); [Coast Reporter: protester arrested](https://coastreporter.net/local-news/protester-arrested-in-clack-creek-forest-3414741)
- Kaslo, 2025: areas along the power line were reserved "for visuals from the highway," with harvesting concentrated upslope. [Med] — [KDCFS December 2025 update](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/december-2025-general-board-meeting-update/)
- Context: BC sets Visual Quality Objectives (VQOs) such as "partial retention." For example, government set a partial-retention VQO for Salisbury Creek in 2014, as described in a Forest Practices Board complaint report. This shows the legal tool behind viewscape disputes. [Med] — [FPB IRC247 Salisbury Creek VQO](https://www.bcfpb.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/IRC247-Salisbury-Creek-VQO.pdf)
- Cheakamus (see next section) adopted small 1–5 ha cutblocks and wider buffers in high-value areas, partly for recreation and cultural values. [High] — [Forest Trends / Ecosystem Marketplace](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/how-two-first-nations-and-a-small-canadian-town-tapped-carbon-finance-to-better-manage-their-shared-forest/)

### Inferences
- SCCF is an honest "consulted but did not change course" card. Listening is not the same as changing the plan, and the community's trust and the board's stability both suffered (see governance section).
- Alternative harvest methods (partial cuts, small patches, visual reserves) show up again and again as the compromise tool: Kaslo, Wetzin'kwa, Cheakamus.

### Gaps
- Nelson (no Nelson community forest was verified in searches), Harrop-Procter viewscape and Williams Lake viewscape examples were not found.
- I did not confirm whether the SCCF Elphinstone block was eventually logged in full, or whether it was modified.

## First Nations partnerships and outcomes

### Takeaway
Four well-documented models: **Williams Lake** (City + Williams Lake First Nation 50/50 limited partnership, CFA signed 2014, 2023 provincial award); **Cheakamus** (Whistler + Squamish Nation + Lil'wat Nation, equal partners since 2009, ecosystem-based management funded by carbon offsets); **Burns Lake** (Village-owned, with Ts'il Kaz Koh, Wet'suwet'en First Nation and the Office of the Wet'suwet'en as partners with equal dividend shares and three of six board seats); and **Cheslatta Carrier Nation** (among the first Indigenous CFA holders, 2002, then rebuilt after the 2018 wildfire). Measured benefits include dividends, grants, carbon revenue and lower harvest intensity.

### Cited Findings
**Williams Lake Community Forest (City of Williams Lake + Williams Lake First Nation / T'exelcemc)**
- March 2014: the province announced the community forest agreement. [High] — [BC Gov News 2014FLNR0018-000272](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2014FLNR0018-000272); [BCCFA: Williams Lake CFA signed](https://bccfa.ca/williams-lake-community-forest-agreement-is-signed)
- Structure: 50/50 limited partnership. Each partner appoints three directors to the general partner company, and the board works at arm's length. About 28,840 ha in two parcels west and east of the city. The UBC wiki gives 2015 as the date the partnership was finalized, which differs from the 2014 agreement date. [Med] — [BCCFA presentation PDF](https://www.bccfa.ca/_files/ugd/b776e1_4f1a20404d5b4f5194d29bf2c1776c31.pdf); [UBC FRST370 wiki](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/The_Williams_Lake_Community_Forest,_British_Columbia,_Canada:_history,_governance_and_lessons_learned)
- 2023: won the Robin Hood Memorial Award for Excellence in Community Forestry. Judges cited relationship-building between the partners and a wildfire fuel-reduction program. 5% of annual profit goes to a community grant program. [High] — [BC Gov News 2023FOR0036-000925](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2023FOR0036-000925); [Williams Lake Tribune](https://www.wltribune.com/news/williams-lake-community-forest-wins-provincial-award-of-excellence-5554960)

**Cheakamus Community Forest (Resort Municipality of Whistler + Squamish Nation + Lil'wat Nation)**
- 2009: 25-year tenure, run by three equal partners through the Cheakamus Community Forest Society (a not-for-profit). [High] — [Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/how-two-first-nations-and-a-small-canadian-town-tapped-carbon-finance-to-better-manage-their-shared-forest/); [RMOW release via BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/?p=1753)
- Decision: the partners adopted an Ecosystem-Based Management (EBM) plan. It means 1–5 ha cutblocks, retained old structure, wider buffers, reduced harvest volume, longer rotations, larger reserves and old-growth protection. Joseph Pallant (Brinkman): "Some parties wanted to continue to have a working forest, while others basically wanted to put a fence around it... The compromise was ecosystem-based management." [High] — [Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/how-two-first-nations-and-a-small-canadian-town-tapped-carbon-finance-to-better-manage-their-shared-forest/); [Brinkman project page](https://www.brinkman.ca/projects/community-led-forest-management-in-cheakamus-community-forest)
- Money: an Improved Forest Management carbon-offset project (with Brinkman and Ecotrust Canada) pays for the cost of EBM. It reduces about 10,000 t CO2e/yr. 2015: Atmospheric Benefits Sharing Agreement with BC, and a first verified issuance of about 44,000 t. Whistler buys the credits to offset its municipal footprint. Trade-off: one board member estimated that 20,000 m³ of harvest given up equals about 15 jobs. [High for structure / Med for numbers] — [Ecotrust Canada briefing PDF](https://ecotrust.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Briefing_CheakamusCarbon.pdf); [Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/how-two-first-nations-and-a-small-canadian-town-tapped-carbon-finance-to-better-manage-their-shared-forest/)

**Burns Lake Community Forest (Village of Burns Lake; partners Ts'il Kaz Koh First Nation [formerly Burns Lake Band], Wet'suwet'en First Nation, Office of the Wet'suwet'en)**
- April 2005: awarded a 25-year Community Forest Agreement (K1A). The forest led mountain pine beetle recovery and planted more than a million trees. [Med] — [BCCFA presentation, Frank Varga](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Burns-Lake-Frank-Varga.pdf)
- Governance: a six-member board, with three public-at-large members and three First Nations representatives. The licence requires profit distribution, and half of profits above a 5% margin go to community and First Nation capacity-building. 2016–17: the Village, Wet'suwet'en First Nation and Ts'il Kaz Koh each received $412,054.92, a total of $1,236,164.76. [Med — single presentation source] — [BCCFA presentation, Frank Varga](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Burns-Lake-Frank-Varga.pdf)
- 2022: local reporting said community benefits grew (amounts not retrieved). [Low] — [Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-comfor-reports-strong-fiscal-year/)

**Cheslatta Carrier Nation (Cheslatta Community Forest)**
- 2002: received a community forest agreement, "among the first, if not the first" Indigenous nation to do so. August 2018: about three-quarters of the community forest burned. The Nation then got $1.25M from the Forest Enhancement Society of BC to salvage and rehabilitate. The Tyee frames this as building a forestry future "from ashes." [Med] — [The Tyee, July 2020](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2020/07/27/First-Nation-Built-Forestry-Future-From-Ashes-Past/); [BCCFA: Cheslatta](https://bccfa.ca/?p=7538)

**Likely Xatśūll Community Forest (Xatśūll First Nation + Likely community / Big Lake)**
- 2017: the province invited Likely-Xatśūll and the Big Lake Community Association to apply jointly for a community forest agreement. Applicants had to show community support. Later Xatśūll job postings (2023) for LXCF board directors show the forest is operating. [Med] — [BC Gov News 2017FLNR0069-000988](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2017FLNR0069-000988); [Williams Lake Tribune](https://www.wltribune.com/news/likely-xatsull-and-big-lake-invited-to-apply-for-community-forest-partnership/); [Xatśūll LXCF board posting](https://www.xatsull.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/LXCF-Director.pdf)

**Esk'etemc**
- Alkali Resource Management, 100% owned by Esk'etemc, manages forest licences, a woodlot and a community forest. No specific decision was found. [Low] — [UBC FRST370 wiki: Esk'etemc](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/2021/_Community_Forest_Management_of_the_Esk%E2%80%99etemc_First_Nation)

**Wetzin'kwa**: Wet'suwet'en representation on the board (see Recreation). [Med]

### Inferences
- Partnership models range from full co-ownership (Williams Lake 50/50, Cheakamus thirds) to a municipality owning the forest with First Nations sharing dividends and seats (Burns Lake). This is a good design choice for students: "Who owns it vs. who decides vs. who gets paid."
- Cheakamus is the clearest case where a First Nations partnership changed harvest decisions (less volume, smaller blocks, longer horizons), with carbon finance making up for some of the lost timber revenue.
- Terminology: use the Nations' current names (Ts'il Kaz Koh First Nation, formerly Burns Lake Band; Williams Lake First Nation, T'exelcemc; Xatśūll First Nation; shíshálh Nation; Squamish Nation / Sḵwx̱wú7mesh Úxwumixw; Lil'wat Nation; Cheslatta Carrier Nation; Esk'etemc). Check spellings with each Nation's own website before printing.

### Gaps
- Measured cultural-value outcomes (e.g., protected cultural sites counted, language/place-name use) were not found for any partnership.
- Nadina Community Forest and Island community forests (e.g., on Vancouver Island) were not researched.
- Current (2023–2026) dividend figures for Burns Lake and Williams Lake were not retrieved.

## Origin stories: why communities fought for a community forest

### Takeaway
Three strong student-friendly origins: **Harrop-Procter** (residents protecting their drinking water since a 1984 meeting, which led to a community co-op tenure), **Revelstoke** (the city bought TFL 56 in 1993 after years of lobbying, approved by referendum), and **Burns Lake** (an early 2000s community forest that became a mountain pine beetle recovery vehicle). Note: the Burns Lake "after mill issues" framing in the brief was not confirmed in my sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Harrop-Procter:** a 1984 public meeting formed a watershed protection committee. After failing to get the area protected as a park, residents moved from "no logging" to seeking a Community Forest Agreement. About 11,300 ha on the West Arm of Kootenay Lake. Run by the Harrop-Procter Community Co-operative, with the watershed society monitoring. Drinking-water protection is the first priority. The forest is FSC-certified. Dates conflict: the minister's invitation in 1997, a pilot/probationary agreement in 1999 or 2000, and a long-term agreement around 2008. [Med; dates Low] — [Soil Association case study](https://soilassociation.org/certification/forestry/case-studies/harrop-procter-community-forest); [BCCFA: Kootenays Part 2](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=2758); [UBC FRST370 case](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/Projects/Forest_Resource_Management_in_Harrop-Procter_Community_Forest,_BC,_Canada); [FPB Kootenay Lake community forests report](https://www.bcfpb.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ARC103-Kootenay-Lake-Community-Forests.pdf)
- **Revelstoke:** April 1993, the City created RCFC and acquired the southern part of former TFL 23 as TFL 56. Reported figures: about 119,000–120,000 ha (about 69,000 ha forested) for about $3M, with a roughly $1M bank loan. Local leaders lobbied about seven years, and a community referendum approved the purchase. At the time only Mission had a comparable community forest. RCFC is wholly city-owned and has been profitable most years, and it created a legacy fund for the city. [Med — note this predates the 2000 window] — [Revelstoke Review: 2013 a milestone year for RCFC](https://www.revelstokereview.com/community/2013-a-milestone-year-for-rcfc/); [Truck Logger BC Winter 2020](https://mydigitalpublication.com/article/Forestry+Community%3A+Revelstoke/4043419/707532/article.html); [Athabasca U case: RCFC repatriates benefits](https://auspace.athabascau.ca/handle/2149/987?show=full); [BCCFA: RCFC legacy fund](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=3721)
- **Burns Lake:** 25-year CFA in April 2005, with pine beetle recovery as a central mission. [Med] — [BCCFA presentation](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Burns-Lake-Frank-Varga.pdf)
- **Cheakamus:** a compromise between "working forest" and "fence around it" factions produced the EBM approach. [High] — [Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/how-two-first-nations-and-a-small-canadian-town-tapped-carbon-finance-to-better-manage-their-shared-forest/)

### Inferences
- Origins tend to come from a threat (to water, to jobs, or to local control) plus years of persistence. The Revelstoke Macpherson case shows the same motive is still alive in 2025–2026.

### Gaps
- Burns Lake's origin and any mill-closure trigger were not confirmed.
- Primary BC government CFA records (agreement numbers and dates) were not retrieved to settle the Harrop-Procter date conflict.

## Governance successes and failures

### Takeaway
I found no clear case of a BC community forest losing its licence or going insolvent. There are three documented governance crises: **McBride** (a $215K deficit in 2015, general manager fired, a council-only board criticized, outside review, more council interventions in 2023), **Cascade Lower Canyon** (partners fired the whole board pending an operational review over grant processes, year unconfirmed), and **Sunshine Coast** (chair, vice-chair and a director resigned amid a court challenge over consultation and claims of council interference). Successes: Williams Lake (2023 award), Revelstoke (long-run profitability), Burns Lake (large dividends).

### Cited Findings
- **McBride Community Forest Corporation:** a $215,000 deficit in 2015. The board fired its general manager in August 2015 and brought in B.A. Blackwell & Associates and the BCCFA (Susan Mulkey) to review operations and governance. Auditors in 2016 said the village had invested $220,856 in the forest. Mayor and council board members stepped down in 2016, and a resident said "From the beginning, it never should have been just mayor and council on the board." A lawsuit with the former manager was settled in 2016. In 2023 the village removed two directors and another resigned in protest, saying the village had "once again" picked its own directors. [Med — mostly one local outlet] — [Rocky Mountain Goat: board loses members (2016)](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2016/08/mcbride-community-forest-board-loses-members/); [Rocky Mountain Goat: McBride operating in the red](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2016/07/mcbride-operating-in-the-red-auditors/); [Rocky Mountain Goat: lawsuit settled](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2016/06/packed-agenda-at-community-forest-meeting/); [TreeFrog: McBride board member resigns](https://treefrogcreative.ca/?p=298611)

### Inferences
- Failure modes in BC look more like **governance breakdowns** (boards made only of politicians, unclear grant rules, councils overriding boards, consultation lawsuits) than licence loss. Honest failure card: "The town council packed the board, money ran out, the manager was fired, and outside reviewers were called in" (McBride).
- The success pattern: arm's-length boards with mixed membership (Williams Lake 3+3, Burns Lake 3 public + 3 First Nations) and transparent profit-sharing rules.

- No verified case of a CFA being cancelled or surrendered, or of a community forest going insolvent, between 2000 and 2026. This could be checked against BC Ministry of Forests tenure records.
