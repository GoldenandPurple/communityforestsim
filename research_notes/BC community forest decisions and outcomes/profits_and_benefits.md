## 1. Dividends and grants: what the money actually built

### Takeaway
Most community forests send profits into a **legacy fund or reserve**, held by the municipality and handed out as grants, rather than into general budgets. Real funded items include:
- a splash park (Revelstoke)
- a Zamboni and rodeo bleachers (Clinton)
- a hall kitchen, seniors' centre equipment and a salmon hatchery intake (Sechelt)
- gardens and a climbing-gym system (Powell River)
- food bank and breakfast programs (Wetzin'kwa)
- a waterfront plan (Nakusp)

## 2. Local jobs and economic benefits (BCCFA Indicators, local mills, value-added)

### Takeaway
BCCFA Indicators surveys consistently show community forests create **more local jobs per cubic metre** than the industry average. The figure rose from about 50% above average (2014) to 63% (2018) to about 76% (2022). But **most community forest logs still go to large mills**, and only a couple run their own mills.

### Cited Findings
- **2022 Indicators** (2021/22 survey of **33 community forests, 80% of operating BCCFA members**): **0.48 full-time local jobs per 1,000 m³** in forestry, logging and support, about **76% above industry average**. (High) — [BCCFA Indicators 2022](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/BCCFA-Indicators-2022-final-web-1.pdf)
- **2018 Indicators:** **0.39 FT jobs/1,000 m³** vs industry **0.24**, i.e. **63% more**. The figure rises to **0.42** when trail building, firefighting and research are counted. (High) — [BCCFA Indicators 2018](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/BCCFA-Indicators-2018-Sept-12-web.pdf); [Exec summary](https://archive.bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/BCCFA-indictors-2018-exec-sum-Sept-5-2018-WEB-final-3.pdf)
- **2014** (first survey): jobs about **50% higher** than industry average. (Medium) — [BCCFA 2014](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/BCCFA-Report-2014-medium_file_A-1.pdf)
- A **2021 BCCFA press release** claimed "more than twice as many jobs/m³" as industry. This is not directly comparable with the 2022 figure of 76%, so cite both cautiously. (Medium) — [2021 press release](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2021-BCCFA-Press-Release-Indicators-Report.pdf)
- A **2020 Indicators** finding: most logs went to very large and large mills, and only **2 of 27** participating community forests ran their own mill. (Medium; via Barriere Star Journal summary) — [Barriere Star Journal](https://www.barrierestarjournal.com/news/lower-north-thompson-community-forest-makes-small-mill-enterprises-work/)
- **Lower North Thompson Community Forest Society (Barriere):**
  - bought land in the **Barriere Industrial Park in 2017**
  - invested **$799,844 in 2018** in land development, equipment and infrastructure
  - runs a **micro-mill** cutting specialty lumber from marginal and oversized Douglas-fir logs
  - started a **firewood business** (partly funded by the BC Rural Dividend) that added jobs
  - only ~**3%** of its logs went to local Woodco in Barriere; most went to Tolko, Interfor, Gilbert Smith and Canfor

  (Medium) — [Barriere Star Journal](https://www.barrierestarjournal.com/news/lower-north-thompson-community-forest-makes-small-mill-enterprises-work/); [BCCFA vignette 2019](https://bccfa.ca/vignette3-2019)
- **BCCFA micro-mill initiative (May 2021 press release).** (Low; title only) — [BCCFA PDF](https://bccfa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/BCCFA-Micro-Mill-Press-Release-21.05.18-1.pdf)
- **McBride Community Forest:** donated about **$3,000 of lumber** to start a local project, which then bought **more than $90,000 of lumber from a one-person mill in McBride**. (Medium; the project type was not stated in the snippet, so verify) — [BCCFA "Adding value to wood and recreation in McBride"](https://bccfa.ca/adding-value-to-wood-and-recreation-in-mcbride)
- **Khowutzun Community Forest** is featured in a BCCFA vignette, "Community Forests bring the benefit of local jobs". (Low; title only) — [BCCFA](https://archive.bccfa.ca/?p=6848)
- **Logan Lake Community Forest:** received **$512,000 FESBC** follow-up funding for fuel treatment identified in its Wildfire Risk Management Plan. During the **2021 Tremont Creek fire**, the fire reached the municipal boundary and "ran out of fuel, saving the town." (High; widely reported) — [Wood Business](https://www.woodbusiness.ca/b-c-community-forest-continues-to-reduce-wildfire-risk-to-the-communities/); [Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Kamloops/344168/Provincial-leaders-say-Logan-Lake-example-shows-value-in-FireSmart-program)

### Inferences
- Honest trade-off for the simulation: community forests win on local jobs per m³, but the value-added "local mill" dream is rare (2 of 27 in 2020). Even LNTCFS, which owns a micro-mill, sells about 97% of its logs to big mills.
- Wildfire fuel work often uses provincial and FESBC money but hires local contractors. This is a non-dividend community benefit with a dramatic real outcome (Logan Lake 2021).

### Gaps
- I could not open the Indicators PDFs, so these figures are missing: % local contractors, total community contributions ($), and value-added sales.

## 5. How boards decide between dividends, reserves and reinvestment

### Takeaway
Boards typically pay out only after funding operations and reserves, and some have rewritten their policies after bad years:
- **Kaslo** replaced a fixed $2/m³ levy with "profitable year + full reserves first".
- **Sechelt** skipped its extraordinary dividend after a 2019 loss but kept paying a regular dividend.
- **Williams Lake** used its first profits partly to repay start-up loans.
- **Valemount** prioritized getting debt-free before investing.

### Cited Findings
- **Kaslo (2024 AGM minutes):** a $2/m³ levy on all wood sold had proved "unrealistic in times of low log prices" and wasn't followed for several years. The new policy makes any dividend depend on a **profitable year**, and **reserve funds must be full** before money is released. (High) — [KDCFS 2024 AGM minutes](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2024-AGM-Minutes.pdf)
- **Sunshine Coast (2020, re: 2019):** after a **2019 loss**, plus a harvest delay while waiting for a new Forest Stewardship Plan (April), SCPI issued **no extraordinary dividend** to the Legacy Fund. It still paid its regular dividend to the District of Sechelt, and legacy-fund grants stayed available from money already banked. (Medium; year inferred) — [Coast Reporter](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/community-forest-posts-loss-hopes-to-resume-logging-in-fall-3415944)
- **Sunshine Coast:** a manager noted prices had yet to recover after the **2018 peak**. (Medium) — same source
- **Revelstoke:** the board pays dividends only from "excess cash not required for immediate operations". Dividends are held in a **reserve for a jointly agreed project** rather than the City's general budget. (Medium) — [BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/?p=3721)
- **Williams Lake (2018):** first payments combined a dividend with **repayment of earlier loans and start-up costs**, and 5% of profit goes to a community grant fund. (High/Medium) — [BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/williams-lake-community-forest-shareholders-reap-first-profits); [UBC wiki](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/The_Williams_Lake_Community_Forest,_British_Columbia,_Canada:_history,_governance_and_lessons_learned)
- **Valemount (2019):** reported "debt-free, able to invest". It had bought part of a Carrier Lumber licence to create the forest. (Medium) — [Rocky Mountain Goat](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2019/05/community-forest-debt-free-able-to-invest/); [BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/congratulations-to-valemount)
- **Powell River:** dividends swing widely with markets: $2.5M (2018), a record $6.26M (2023), ~$3.7M (2024). The **City reserve fund** smooths grant-giving. (Medium) — [Powell River Peak](https://www.prpeak.com/local-news/powell-river-community-forest-expenditure-approved-10912332)
- **Clinton:** profits are split between the Village (~2/3, toward a Public Works yard) and community group grants (~1/3) (2020). (High) — [BCCFA](https://bccfa.ca/clinton-community-forest-disburses-grants-of-more-than-250000)
