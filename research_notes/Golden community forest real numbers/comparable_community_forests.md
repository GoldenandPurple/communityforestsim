# Comparable BC Community Forests: Finances, Governance and Carbon

**Method note (read first):** The research environment blocked WebFetch to almost every relevant domain (bccfa.ca, nakuspcommunityforest.com, thenarwhal.ca, biv.com, timescolonist.com, treefrogcreative.ca, woodbusiness.ca and others). So every figure below comes from **web search result excerpts** of the cited pages, not from reading the full documents. Confidence ratings reflect this. Before a number goes into the simulation as a hard anchor, a human should open the cited URL and check it. No full audited financial statements (income statement or balance sheet line items) could be read for any community forest. Operating-cost breakdowns (staff, roads, planning, insurance) are therefore a major gap.

## Q1. Per-forest figures: AAC, area, revenue, costs, net income, reserves, dividends

### Takeaway
Small interior and Kootenay community forests (15,000–25,000 m3/yr AAC, 10,000–18,000 ha) typically pay the owning municipality $25k–$600k a year in dividends, which swing a lot from year to year, plus about $50k–$80k a year in small community grants. Nakusp (NACFOR: 20,000 m3, 12,626 ha) is the closest well-documented analogue for a Golden-scale forest. Larger tenures (Revelstoke about 78,500–90,000 m3; Valemount about 95,000 m3; Burns Lake up to 260,000 m3) produce revenue in the millions, with dividends of $0.3M–$1.5M+ per partner.

### Cited Findings

**Nakusp and Area Community Forest (NACFOR). Best small-scale analogue.**
- Governance: a BC corporation 100% owned by the Village of Nakusp, run by a volunteer board of directors. Its 25-year Community Forest Agreement covers **12,626 ha** with an average harvest of **20,000 m3/yr**. Confidence: high. — [NACFOR Dividends & Legacy Fund / FAQ](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/); [NACFOR FAQ](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/faq/)
- Dividend of **$250,000** to the Village in 2025, presented at the November 10 council meeting. Confidence: high. — [NACFOR: $250,000 Dividend to Village of Nakusp in 2025](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/2025-dividend-to-village-of-nakusp/)
- Dividend of **$465,000** declared in 2019, based on 2018 results. This was the second-largest ever; the largest was **over $582,000 in 2014**. Cumulative giving to the community was **more than $1.5 million** from formation in 2008 to 2019. The 2019 dividend was "almost half as much as the village raises through taxes". Matching it through property tax would have needed an increase of more than 45%. Confidence: medium-high. — [Arrow Lakes News, "NACFOR 2019 dividend big boost to Nakusp village budget"](https://arrowlakesnews.com/news/nacfor-2019-dividend-big-boost-to-nakusp-village-budget)
- NACFOR president Kathy Smith attributed the large 2019 dividend to "successful operations, planning, management, markets, marketing, and costs" in 2018. — [Arrow Lakes News](https://arrowlakesnews.com/news/nacfor-2019-dividend-big-boost-to-nakusp-village-budget)
- Donations program: since 2014 the board has awarded about **$50,000/yr** in donations to groups in the Arrow Lakes region (Nakusp and RDCK Area K). Confidence: high. — [NACFOR Dividends & Legacy Fund](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/)
- Legacy Fund: Village of Nakusp Bylaw 653 (2014) created the NACFOR Legacy Fund. Dividends are held there "for future distribution" to projects with broad, lasting community benefit. NACFOR's stated objective is to pay out profits "net of operating capital", which implies it keeps operating reserves first. — [NACFOR Dividends & Legacy Fund](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/)

### Inferences
- A Golden-scale forest (about 15,000–25,000 m3/yr, 10,000–20,000 ha) can be modelled on NACFOR, Kaslo and Creston. Plausible ranges: annual dividends of about **$0–600k** (a median year about $250k), a donations and grants budget of about **$50–80k/yr**, and **loss years** now and then (SCCF 2019) when harvest is delayed or markets drop.
- The documented dividends are very volatile: NACFOR went $582k (2014), $465k (2019), $250k (2025), and SCCF went from +$1.2M to −$0.39M in one year. The simulation should use volatile, market-linked revenue.
- The Burns Lake margin (net profit about 53% of gross in FY2020–21) was a lumber-price boom year and should not be used as a normal margin.
- Common governance pattern: a municipality-owned corporation, dividends sent to a council-controlled Legacy Fund (Nakusp, Revelstoke, Sechelt), plus a separate small donations program run by the board.

- No audited line-item operating costs (staff, roads, planning and FSP, insurance, admin) were found for any forest, because full PDFs could not be fetched. - No cash reserve or retained-earnings figures were found for any forest.

- Harrop-Procter's 2,600 m3 AAC may be outdated.

## Q2. BCCFA Indicators reports: sector averages

### Takeaway
The newest report is the **2026 Indicators Report** (reporting year 2025, released September 2026). It found **more than $129M in economic activity**, **1,833 people** earning all or part of their income from community forests, and **37 respondents (77% of operating members)**. Job creation per m3 runs about **74–85% above the industry average**, depending on the year. Older reports give per-forest averages of about **$1.84M economic activity** and **$558k cash and in-kind community contributions**.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 report (reporting year 2025):** more than **$129 million** in economic activity. **1,833 people** earned all or part of their income through community forest activities. Members provided "nearly three-quarters more" forestry services (jobs) than the industry average. Data came from **37 member community forests (77% of operating members)**, measured across 18 indicators. Confidence: medium (from news and press-release excerpts). — [102.9 Rewind Radio, Sept 17, 2026](https://1029rewindradio.ca/2026/09/17/new-report-shows-more-than-129m-generated-from-b-c-community-forests-in-2025/); [Tree Frog Creative, 2026 Indicators Report](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-2026-indicators-report-measuring-the-benefits-of-community-forestry/)
- A search summary linked an investment of **$2.5 million in wildfire resiliency** and **over 2,800 hours** of community education to the recent reports. Which report year these figures belong to was not confirmed. Confidence: low-medium. — [BCCFA Indicators page](https://www.bccfa.ca/indicatorsreport)
- Cumulative wildfire figures: community forests invested **over $8 million of their own money** and managed **over $17 million from outside sources** on wildfire mitigation (the "past decade" framing in the 2025 report, though the exact report was not confirmed). Confidence: low-medium. — [BCCFA Indicators page](https://www.bccfa.ca/indicatorsreport); [BCCFA 2025 report launch](https://www.bccfa.ca/post/bccfa-launches-2025-indicators-report-and-new-brand)
- **2025 report (reporting year 2024):** 33 respondents (70% of operating members). Full-time jobs in forestry, logging and support services were created at a rate **82% higher** than industry. — [BCCFA 2025 launch](https://www.bccfa.ca/post/bccfa-launches-2025-indicators-report-and-new-brand); [Tree Frog Creative](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-releases-2025-indicators-report/)
- The **2024 report** was the 10th-anniversary edition. — [Creston Community Forest repost](https://www.crestoncommunityforest.com/post/bccfa-community-forest-indicators-report-2024)
- **2020 report (via National Observer, Oct 26, 2020):** jobs per m3 **85% above** industry. Average per community forest was **$1.84M** in economic activity and **$558,309** in cash and in-kind contributions to local projects. Wildfire management investment had **tripled**, above legal requirements. Confidence: medium-high. — [National Observer](https://www.nationalobserver.com/2020/10/26/news/community-forest-dividends-go-well-beyond-logging-profits)
- An older study (SIBAC/BC Rural Centre, 2017) found that 23 community forests had together put **$17.3M** of profits back into their communities since 2003. — [BC Rural Centre report](https://bcruralcentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/SIBAC-Community-Forests-Community-Benefits-Final-Report-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- $129M across 37 forests is about **$3.5M of economic activity per forest per year** in 2025. This is the gross economic activity measure, not profit.
- 1,833 people across 37 forests is about 50 people per forest earning some income. Most are part-time or contractors.
- For the simulation, about **$0.5M/yr** in cash and in-kind community contributions is a reasonable sector average for a mid-size community forest (2020 report). Smaller forests like NACFOR sit well below it.

### Gaps
- Could not read the 2026 or 2025 report PDFs, so I have no current per-m3 community-investment figures, no current average wildfire spending per forest, and no current total cash community contributions.

## Q3. Governance models and First Nations partnerships

### Takeaway
BC has **62 Community Forest Agreements**. **13** are held solely by First Nations and **20** in partnership with First Nations, so **33 (more than half)** involve First Nations. Holders include municipality-owned corporations (Nakusp, Revelstoke, Valemount, Burns Lake, Sechelt), non-profit societies (Kaslo), co-ops (Harrop-Procter), multi-party non-profit corporations (Creston) and municipal–First Nation partnerships (Williams Lake, Cheakamus, Burns Lake).

### Cited Findings
- 62 CFAs are operating. 13 are held solely by First Nations and 20 in partnership, for 33 in total (more than 50%). More than 100 First Nations and rural communities are involved. Confidence: medium-high. — [BCCFA / 2026 Indicators coverage](https://www.bccfa.ca/community-forestry); [Tree Frog Creative 2026](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-2026-indicators-report-measuring-the-benefits-of-community-forestry/)
- Municipality-owned corporations with volunteer boards: NACFOR (Village of Nakusp) — [NACFOR](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/faq/); RCFC (City of Revelstoke) — [RCFC](https://rcfc.bc.ca/); VCF (Village of Valemount) — [VCF](https://valemountcommunityforest.ca/about/)
- Society: Kaslo & District Community Forest Society. — [KDCFS](https://kaslocommunityforest.org/about-the-kaslo-district-community-forest-society/)
- Co-op with no dividends: Harrop-Procter. — [HPCF Co-op](https://hpcommunityforest.org/the-co-op/)
- Multi-stakeholder board including an environmental NGO: Creston (Town, RDCK, Economic Development Commission, Wildsight). — [BC Gov 2008](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2005-2009/2008FOR0145-001668.htm)
- Municipal–First Nation partnerships:
  - Williams Lake: City and WLFN. — [City of Williams Lake](http://www.williamslake.ca/483/Williams-Lake-Community-Forest)
  - Burns Lake: Village as shareholder, with Wet'suwet'en and Ts'il Kaz Koh as equal profit partners. — [Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-comfor-reports-strong-fiscal-year-6309850)
  - Cheakamus Community Forest Society: RMOW, Lil'wat Nation and Squamish Nation, since April 2009. — [Cheakamus About](https://cheakamuscommunityforest.com/about/)
- CFAs are 25-year replaceable tenures, with timber priced by tabular stumpage. — [BC Gov CFA page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/community-forest-agreements)

### Inferences
- A realistic Golden setup could be a Town of Golden-owned corporation, possibly partnered with local First Nations (e.g., Ktunaxa or Secwépemc communities, though this was not researched here). Equal profit splits among partners, as at Burns Lake, are a documented model.

### Gaps

## Q4. Carbon offsets in BC community forests

### Takeaway
**Cheakamus Community Forest** (Whistler) is essentially the only BC community forest that has sold offsets under the provincial system. A much smaller Quadra Island project is the only other site that has sold credits under the government market. Cheakamus halved its harvest (AAC 21,000 m3 instead of 41,170 m3) and made about **$100k/yr** from **10,000–15,000 tCO2e/yr** at about **$30/t** on the voluntary market. Under BC's output-based pricing system, sales rose **six-fold in 2025**, including a single purchase of more than **$600k** by Newmont. It now reportedly makes more from carbon than from logging. Other community forests are blocked by protocol complexity and provincial steady-harvest requirements. The BCCFA calls the carbon route a "dead end".

### Cited Findings
- Cheakamus: **more than 33,000 ha**. The EBM-based AAC is **21,000 m3/yr**, versus **41,170 m3/yr** under standard management. Openings are 0.5–5 ha with 5–30% retention. Confidence: high. — [Cheakamus FAQ / EBM plan](https://cheakamuscommunityforest.com/ecosystem-based-management-plan/); [BC Gov 2015 release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015FLNR0075-000574)
- Offsets: **10,000–15,000 tCO2e/yr** verified, giving about **$100,000/yr** in revenue. This supports management above standards and a harvest reduction of nearly 50%. The project has sold offsets since 2009 and was the first of its kind in BC's carbon market. Confidence: high. — [Ecotrust Canada](https://ecotrust.ca/priorities/climate/cheakamus-community-forest-carbon-offsets/); [Ecotrust briefing PDF](https://ecotrust.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Briefing_CheakamusCarbon.pdf)
- 2015: Cheakamus and the Province signed a carbon offset purchase agreement. About **80%** of offsets were sold to the Province, with the rest going to voluntary buyers such as RMOW and the SLRD. — [BC Gov 2015 release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015FLNR0075-000574); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-forests-carbon-offsets-cheakamus/)
- 2025: carbon credit sales **spiked six-fold**. Newmont bought **more than $600,000** of credits for its Red Chris mine. The price was about **$30/t** on the voluntary market and "more than doubled" under BC's output-based pricing system (OBPS). Cheakamus now reportedly makes more from carbon credits than from harvesting. Confidence: medium-high. — [Business in Vancouver](https://www.biv.com/news/resources-agriculture/new-carbon-pricing-fuels-600-revenue-surge-for-bc-community-forest-12259045); [Coast Reporter repost](https://www.coastreporter.net/resources-agriculture/new-carbon-pricing-fuels-600-revenue-surge-for-bc-community-forest-12259045)
- Only Cheakamus and a much smaller Quadra Island project have sold credits under the government market, despite 62 community forests. Provincial protocols have not kept up with other markets, and the Province failed to harmonize with Quebec and California in 2008. A BCCFA senior manager said: "It's just such a dead end for us. It's so frustrating. It does not make sense." Cheakamus's success was driven by Doug Konkin, a former deputy minister of forests. Confidence: medium-high. — [BIV, "Experts warn BC's forest carbon market hitting a 'dead end'"](https://www.biv.com/news/environment/experts-warn-bcs-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-12291061); [Castanet repost](https://www.castanet.net/news/BC/615307/Experts-warn-B-C-s-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-)
- Barrier example: a 2014 study of a Nuxalk Nation carbon project near Bella Coola found several obstacles, including the **provincial requirement that the community forest keep a steady timber harvest**. — [BIV "dead end"](https://www.biv.com/news/environment/experts-warn-bcs-forest-carbon-market-hitting-a-dead-end-12291061)
- The Province (energy ministry spokesperson) says the offset steps (real, verifiable, permanent) "may be difficult for some community forests to demonstrate". Approval depends on "site-specific conditions" that are "not easily replicated using a single model". — [Times Colonist, "Getting credit"](https://www.timescolonist.com/islander/getting-credit-why-havent-more-bc-community-forests-benefited-from-carbon-credits-12321769)
- BC's offset development requirements. — [BC Gov, Developing emission offset projects](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/climate-change/industry/offset-projects/develop); [BC Gov, Forest carbon emission offsets](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/natural-resource-stewardship/natural-resources-climate-change/natural-resources-climate-change-mitigation/forest-carbon-emission-offsets)
- Related: a UBC Sustainability paper on forest restoration, carbon offsets and Indigenous cultural burning covers the tension between offsets and fire and fuel treatments. It was not read. — [UBC 2023 paper](https://sustain.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/2023-017_ForestCarbonOffsetsIndigenousBurning_Stefanuk.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Simulation carbon option.** Deferring about 50% of AAC (about 20,000 m3/yr at Cheakamus) produced about 10–15k tCO2e/yr worth about $100k/yr at $30/t. Under OBPS it could be about $200k–$600k+ in a strong year. So the harvest forgone at Cheakamus was roughly 1.3–2 m3 per tonne credited.
- **Barriers to model.** A Golden-type forest would face high up-front validation costs, a multi-year approval process and a real risk of rejection. The steady-harvest requirement also creates a policy conflict.
- **Wildfire conflict.** Fuel treatments remove biomass, which cuts stored carbon, and wildfire is a permanence risk for offsets. No direct documented BC case of a carbon contract blocking fuel treatment was found. This is a plausible simulation tension, but not a sourced event.

### Gaps
- No up-front project development or verification cost figures were found for Cheakamus.
- No sourced example was found of a carbon contract conflicting with wildfire fuel treatment in a BC community forest.
- No details were found on the Quadra Island project.
- Harrop-Procter carbon activity was not confirmed.

## Q5. Profit distribution debates: dividends vs reserves vs reinvestment

### Takeaway
The documented models are:
- 50/50 dividend and retention (SCCF)
- dividends net of operating capital to a council Legacy Fund, plus a separate donations program of about $50k (NACFOR)
- loan repayment before dividends (Williams Lake)
- a council policy splitting profits among general revenue, a Forest Reserve Fund and a Legacy Fund (North Cowichan's municipal forest, which is not a CFA)
- no dividends at all, with all surplus reinvested (the Harrop-Procter co-op)

### Cited Findings
- SCCF: about 50% of profit to the District and 50% retained. All dividends go to the Legacy Fund. In a loss year it pays only the small regular dividend. — [Coast Reporter](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/more-than-500000-headed-to-sechelt-from-community-forest-5303454); [Coast Reporter 2020](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/community-forest-posts-loss-hopes-to-resume-logging-in-fall-3415944)
- NACFOR: profits "net of operating capital" go to the Village as dividends and are held in the Legacy Fund (Bylaw 653). The board runs its own donations program of about $50k/yr. The 2019 dividend was nearly half the Village's tax levy, which shows the pressure on councils to use dividends as general revenue. — [NACFOR](https://www.nakuspcommunityforest.com/dividends-legacy-fund/); [Arrow Lakes News](https://arrowlakesnews.com/news/nacfor-2019-dividend-big-boost-to-nakusp-village-budget)
- Williams Lake: the first payouts (2018) combined dividends with repayment of start-up loans. — [WL Tribune](https://wltribune.com/news/williams-lake-community-forest-shareholders-reap-first-profits)
- North Cowichan (municipal forest reserve, not a CFA): an average of about $70,000 went to general revenue, with the rest going to the Forest Reserve Fund and Forest Legacy Fund under the Council's Allocation of Profits Policy. It also went through a public debate on whether to harvest at all. — [North Cowichan council report](https://pub-northcowichan.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=21438); [The Discourse](https://thediscourse.ca/cowichan-valley/cowichan-community-forest)
- Harrop-Procter: pays no dividends and puts surplus into ecosystem-based management and value-added processing. — [HPCF](https://hpcommunityforest.org/the-co-op/)
- Burns Lake: equal three-way split between the Village and two First Nations, plus donations and a legacy fund distribution. — [Lakes District News](https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/news/burns-lake-comfor-reports-strong-fiscal-year-6309850); [blcomfor.com](https://blcomfor.com/community/)
- Revelstoke: dividends to a council-managed Legacy Fund, plus direct donations to non-profits. — [Revelstoke Mountaineer](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/brief-revelstoke-community-forestry-corporation-legacy-fund-gets-a-700000-boost/)
- SCCF has faced conservation opposition to logging specific stands (the "Chanterelle Forest" and a park proposal), which is a harvest-versus-conservation debate. — [Coast Reporter, ELF urges council](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-news/elf-urges-sechelt-council-to-save-chanterelle-forest-3388589); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/weve-been-cheated-sunshine-coast-community-braces-for-logging-of-forest-at-heart-of-park-proposal/)

### Inferences
- These give natural simulation levers: the dividend payout ratio (0–100%), the size of the operating reserve, the donations budget, and whether council may move Legacy Fund money into general revenue.

### Gaps
