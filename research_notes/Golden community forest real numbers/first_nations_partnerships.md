## 1. Which nations assert territory around Golden / Kicking Horse / Mount 7, and how do local institutions word their acknowledgements?

### Takeaway
Local institutions in Golden consistently name **two First Nations, the Ktunaxa and the Secwépemc, whose territory is "unceded"**, and add the **Métis as having "chosen" the area as home**. In Golden the Métis are usually named through the **Métis Nation Columbia River Society** (the local chartered community of Métis Nation BC). Within the Secwépemc Nation, the community most directly involved around Golden is the **Shuswap Band (Kenpesq't)**, based near Invermere. The Shuswap Band is the First Nation partner in the proposed **Kenpesq't Community Forest** around Golden. I found no Stoney Nakoda claim in any Golden-area acknowledgement. (High confidence on the pattern, medium on exact wording.)

### Cited Findings
**Acknowledgement wording (as it appears in search snippets):**
- **Town of Golden:** "The Town of Golden is located on the traditional unceded territory of the Ktunaxa and Secwépemc peoples, the chosen home of Métis heritage and other diverse Indigenous peoples." The search engine linked this to the Town's draft Official Community Plan (Bylaw No. 1516, 2026). — [Town of Golden OCP Bylaw 1516, 2026 (PDF)](https://www.golden.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/Bylaw%201516%202026%20Town%20of%20Golden%20Bylaw%20Official%20Community%20Plan%20PH.pdf) *(medium confidence: snippet only, full PDF not read)*
- **Tourism Golden:** "grateful to live, work and play on the traditional unceded territory of the Ktunaxa and Secwepemc peoples which is also the chosen home to the Métis Nation Columbia River Society. [We] respectfully acknowledge the culture and history that connects these Nations with this land; and their wisdom, sacrifices, and stewardship of the past, the present, and the future." Tourism Golden also says "Three Nations have deep connections to this landscape: the Ktunaxa Nation, the Secwepemc Nation, and the Métis Nation." — [Tourism Golden: Ktunaxa Nation](https://www.tourismgolden.com/blog/ktunaxa-nation); [Tourism Golden: Indigenous Peoples](https://www.tourismgolden.com/about/indigenous-peoples)

The Band says it "has lived in the area around Golden and the Columbia Valley since time immemorial." — [Shuswap Band: About](https://www.shuswapband.net/about-shuswap-band/); [IAAC Chapter 24 – Shuswap Band (Kenpesq't) (PDF)](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80087/155159E.pdf)

- **Ktunaxa territory:** described as including "the Kootenay, Flathead, and Columbia River watersheds within the area extending from the Arrow and Kinbasket Lakes east to the Alberta border." That range reaches the Golden area. — [BC Treaty Commission: Ktunaxa Nation](https://bctreaty.ca/ktunaxa-nation/); [Ktunaxa/Kinbasket Statement of Intent map (PDF)](https://bctreaty.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Ktunaxa_Kinbasket_SOI_Map.pdf)

- **An older provincial-style guide lists different nations:** it gives Golden as "Ktunaxa, Okanagan and Shuswap." That differs from every current local acknowledgement, which name only Ktunaxa and Secwépemc. — [CNC Traditional Territories of B.C. guide (PDF)](https://cnc.bc.ca/docs/default-source/default-document-library/indigenous-territory-guide.pdf?sfvrsn=60123e83_0)

### Inferences
- The safest acknowledgement for the simulation follows the **Town of Golden / SD6 pattern**: "the unceded territory of the Ktunaxa and Secwépemc peoples, and the chosen home of the Métis." Students in SD6 schools will already know this form.
- The "Kinbasket" name (as in "Ktunaxa Kinbasket Treaty Council") reflects historical ties between the Shuswap Band and the Ktunaxa. The Shuswap Band is not a member of the Ktunaxa Nation Council and runs the Golden community-forest initiative itself. A facilitator should avoid saying the Shuswap Band is "part of Ktunaxa."
- The older "Okanagan" entry for Golden probably reflects outdated broad-brush mapping. Do not use it.
- **Territory overlaps.** Ktunaxa and Secwépemc territories overlap around Golden, and the two nations may describe the boundary differently. In the simulation, do not draw a line or decide whose territory is "stronger."

## 2. Correct names, spellings, diacritics and terms to avoid

### Takeaway
Use the spellings below. Where a community gives its own name, use that name, with an English name in brackets if helpful. "Shuswap" survives in official names (Shuswap Band; the Columbia Shuswap Regional District) but is an exonym (a name given by outsiders). Use "Secwépemc" for the people and nation. (Medium–high confidence.)

### Cited Findings
- **Secwépemc** (people/nation), pronounced roughly "shəkh-whep-MIKH." "Shuswap" is the exonym. **Secwepemctsín** is the language. **Secwepemcúl̓ecw** (often written Secwepemcúlecw) is the territory, about 145,000 km². — [Wikipedia: Secwépemc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secw%C3%A9pemc); [TRU Secwépemc pronunciations](https://www.tru.ca/indigenous/indigenous-education-team/pronunciations.html); [Secwepemc Strong: Secwepemctsín](https://secwepemcstrong.com/secwepemc-governance-4-pillars-overview/secwepemcstin/)
- **Kukpi7** is the Secwépemc title for Chief. It is used in coverage of Shuswap Kukpi7 Barb Cote. — [e-Know: Shuswap Band and Canfor sign relationship agreement](https://www.e-know.ca/regions/east-kootenay/shuswap-band-and-canfor-sign-relationship-agreement/)
- **Kenpesq't** (sometimes written Kenpesq’t with a curly apostrophe) is the Shuswap Band's own name. — [Town of Golden: Kenpesq't Community Forest](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest)
- **Ktunaxa** (pronounced roughly "k-too-NAH-ha"; pronunciation not verified from a primary source). Community names use the glottal-stop letter ʔ and a lowercase first letter: ʔaq̓am, ʔakisq̓nuk, Yaqan nuʔkiy, Yaq̓it ʔa·knuqⱡi'it. **ʔamakʔis Ktunaxa** means the Ktunaxa homelands and appears in Ktunaxa land-stewardship materials. The older exonym "Kootenay/Kutenai" survives in place names. — [Ktunaxa Communities](https://www.ktunaxa.org/contact-2/communities/); [ʔamakʔis Ktunaxa Resource Atlas](https://ktunaxahakqyit.org/26256/widgets/184718/documents/133387); [Wikipedia: Kutenai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutenai)
- Local bodies write **"the Ktunaxa and Secwépemc peoples"** (plural "peoples" for more than one nation) and **"Métis Nation Columbia River Society,"** with é in Métis. — [Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/blog/ktunaxa-nation); [MNBC](https://www.mnbc.ca/citizens-culture/chartered-communities/metis-nation-columbia-river-society)

### Inferences
- Terms and practices to avoid:
  - Do not use "Shuswap people" or "Kootenay Indians" for the peoples.
  - Do not use "Indians," except in legal names such as "Shuswap Indian Band."
  - Do not use possessives such as "our First Nations" or "Canada's Indigenous peoples," which imply ownership.
  - Do not use "tribe" as a generic term.
  - Do not use "stakeholder" for rights-holding nations; use "rights holders" or "Indigenous governments."
  - Do not use past-tense framing such as "used to live here."
  - Do not describe the Métis as holding "unceded territory" in the same sense as First Nations; local usage says "chosen home."
- **Capitalization:** "Indigenous," "First Nations," "Métis" and "Nation" (in proper names) are capitalized.
- **Letters must survive fonts and copy-paste.** ʔ and q̓ can render as boxes or be stripped. Test the printed materials and, if needed, use the English forms with a note, e.g. "ʔakisq̓nuk (Akisqnuk)."

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a KNC or Shuswap Band style guide or official pronunciation guide (proxy blocked). The pronunciations above are secondary.
- I found no primary explanation of how "Kenpesq't" should be pronounced.

## 3. Current forestry agreements, treaty status and land-use planning around Golden

### Takeaway
The most important real precedent is local. Since a **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) signed on 21 November 2022**, the **Shuswap Band, the Town of Golden and CSRD Area A (Rural Golden)** have worked together toward the **Kenpesq't Community Forest** in the Golden Timber Supply Area. This is a three-government partnership whose name comes from the First Nation. Separately, the Shuswap Band recently signed a relationship agreement with Canfor. The **Ktunaxa (Ktunaxa Kinbasket Treaty Council) are in Stage 5 of the BC treaty process** under a 2018 rights-recognition MOU; no Final Agreement was found. (High confidence on the MOU; medium on current status.)

### Cited Findings
- **Kenpesq't Community Forest MOU:** "More than 20 years of work culminated in a historic signing on November 21 [2022], when representatives of the Shuswap Band, Town of Golden and Columbia Shuswap Regional District Area A Rural Golden met at the Shuswap Band office near Invermere." The ceremony was hosted by the Shuswap Band and "opened with a prayer followed by the Shuswap Band Drumming Ceremony." — [Golden Star: Town of Golden celebrates historic moment 20 years in the making](https://thegoldenstar.net/home/town-of-golden-celebrates-historic-moment-20-years-in-the-making/); [Town of Golden: 2022 year in review](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/news-notices/town-golden-year-review-highlights-2022)
- The three governments "reaffirmed their desire to come together and better understand one another while working towards the creation of the 'Kenpesq't Community Forest' within the Golden Timber Supply Area." The forest is to be "within the Shuswap Band's traditional territory." — [Town of Golden: Kenpesq't Community Forest](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest)
- The work is led by the **Golden & Area Community Forest Team (GACFT)**, with representatives from all three communities. — [Golden & Area Community Forest Initiative](https://goldenareacf.com/); [Town of Golden: Golden and Area Community Forest Initiative](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/golden-and-area-community-forest-initiative); [BCCFA: Bringing a community forest to Golden](https://bccfa.ca/bringing-a-community-forest-to-golden/)
- **How the tenure works:** the Province directly invites communities to apply for a Community Forest Agreement (CFA) without competition. CFAs run 25–99 years and are replaceable every 10 years. — [Province of BC: Community Forest Agreements](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/community-forest-agreements)
- **Kenpesq't forestry businesses:** Kenpesq't Holdings has a board of directors, and Kenpesq't Territorial Operations Ltd. (KTO) was established in 2019. A federal procurement record from February 2025 names "Kenpesq't Forestry Limited Partners." — [Shuswap Band: Kenpesq't Holdings board](https://www.shuswapband.net/kenpesqt-board-of-directors/); [Kenpesq't Holdings: About](https://kenpesqt.ca/about-us/); [Open Canada contract record](https://rechercher.ouvert.canada.ca/contracts/record/pc%2CC-2024-2025-Q4-00631) *(medium confidence, snippet only)*
- **Shuswap Band–Canfor relationship agreement:** signed by Shuswap Kukpi7 Barb Cote and Canfor VP Matthew Parras "on Thursday, Sept. 3." It covers forest stewardship, community initiatives and economic development. Councillor Mark Thomas, who holds the Band's forestry portfolio: "stewardship comes before economics." — [e-Know](https://www.e-know.ca/regions/east-kootenay/shuswap-band-and-canfor-sign-relationship-agreement/); [Columbia Valley Pioneer](https://www.columbiavalleypioneer.com/shuswap-band-canfor-sign-agreement) *(year not shown in the snippet; 3 Sept fell on a Thursday in 2026, so this is probably September 2026, but unverified)*
- The Shuswap Band also has a relationship agreement with BC Hydro and a land-stewardship initiative ("United front to return lands to Shuswap stewardship"). — [Columbia Valley Pioneer: Shuswap Band, BC Hydro](https://www.columbiavalleypioneer.com/shuswap-band-bc-hydro-sign-historic-relationship-agreement/); [Columbia Valley Pioneer: United front](https://www.columbiavalleypioneer.com/united-front-to-return-lands-to-shuswap-stewardship/)
- **Ktunaxa treaty:** "The Ktunaxa Nation… (KKTC) treaty table is in Stage 5 negotiations," reached through the "Ktunaxa Nation Rights Recognition & Core Treaty Memorandum of Understanding" (signed 30 Nov 2018). That MOU sets out a "core" treaty with supplemental side agreements. — [BC Treaty Commission: Ktunaxa Nation](https://bctreaty.ca/ktunaxa-nation/); [BC Gov news 2018: Canada, B.C., Ktunaxa move closer to treaty](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2018IRR0074-002326)
- There is also a **Ktunaxa Incremental Treaty Agreement (Creston)**. — [KNC Creston ITA (PDF)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/natural-resource-stewardship/consulting-with-first-nations/agreements/knc_creston_ita_-_signed.pdf)
- **Caution on the Columbia River Treaty:** one search summary mixed the BC treaty with **Columbia River Treaty** modernization. That Canada–US process (with Ktunaxa, Secwépemc and Syilx participation) produced an agreement in principle in July 2024 covering ecosystem health, salmon restoration and reservoir fluctuations. It is a different process from the BC treaty. — [Ktunaxa Nation: March 2025 posts](https://www.ktunaxa.org/2025/03/) *(medium confidence; not read in full)*
- **Background on Golden forestry:** a June 2026 presentation in Golden covered a new Forest Act. — [Golden Chamber: New Forest Act presentation](https://www.goldenchamber.bc.ca/2026/05/26/new-forest-act-june-2026/)

### Inferences
- The real Golden story is: **First Nation name, three governments, a signed MOU, and a long lead time (20+ years).** It can anchor the Partnership round directly. Students can negotiate *how* a partnership like this might be governed without playing the Shuswap Band.
- The snippets did not show whether the Province has issued the Kenpesq't CFA or how the three partners would split governance (board seats, ownership shares). Present it as "in progress" unless verified.
- Kinbasket Reservoir, created by the Mica Dam and the Columbia River Treaty, flooded Secwépemc and Ktunaxa lands and is a sensitive history. Mention it only if sourced and framed carefully.

## 4. Real BC examples of First Nations partnerships and co-management in community forests

### Takeaway
BC community forests show several governance models:
- **Equal partnership through a society with equal board seats:** Cheakamus (Squamish, Líl̓wat, Whistler).
- **50/50 limited partnership between a city and a First Nation:** Williams Lake Community Forest (T'exelcemc and the City).
- **Company with reserved First Nation board seats:** Burns Lake.
- **Two partners each holding volume and combining it:** Likely–Xatśūll with Big Lake.
- **First Nation-only area-based tenure:** the First Nations Woodland Licence.

The BCCFA reports that nearly half of BC community forests are run by First Nations or by First Nation–community partnerships. (High confidence.)

### Cited Findings
**Sector-wide figures**
- "Nearly half of the community forests in BC are operated by First Nations or by partnerships between First Nations and neighbouring non-indigenous communities." BC has **62 CFAs**, involving "more than 100 First Nations and rural communities." — [BCCFA Indicators Report page](https://www.bccfa.ca/indicatorsreport); [Tree Frog Creative: 2026 Indicators Report](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-2026-indicators-report-measuring-the-benefits-of-community-forestry/)
- The 2026 Indicators Report (reporting on 2025) found **more than $129M in economic activity** and **1,833 people** earning all or part of their income from community forests. Data came from 37 members (77% of operating members), across 18 indicators including "First Nations relationships" and wildfire resilience. — [102.9 Rewind Radio, 17 Sept 2026](https://1029rewindradio.ca/2026/09/17/new-report-shows-more-than-129m-generated-from-b-c-community-forests-in-2025/); [Tree Frog Creative](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-2026-indicators-report-measuring-the-benefits-of-community-forestry/)

**Cheakamus Community Forest (Whistler)**
- A "three-way equal partnership between the Squamish Nation, Lil'wat Nation and the Resort Municipality of Whistler."
- Established in April 2009 with a 25-year tenure, and managed through the Cheakamus Community Forest Society, "an independent not-for-profit organization."
- The board gives "equal standing for all three groups."
- It covers about 36,000 ha, is managed under an **ecosystem-based management** plan reflecting all three partners' land-use plans, harvests at a "significant reduction… from historical levels," and has a carbon-offset agreement with BC (2015).

— [Cheakamus CF: About](https://cheakamuscommunityforest.com/about/); [UBC Wiki: Cheakamus CF](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST270/Wiki_Projects/Cheakamus_Community_Forest_in_Whistler_British_Columbia); [BC gov 2009 release](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2005-2009/2009FOR0079-000869.htm); [BC gov 2015 carbon offset release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2015FLNR0075-000574)

**Williams Lake Community Forest**
- An "equal (50/50) partnership between the T'exelcemc (Williams Lake Indian Band) and the City of Williams Lake," held through a limited partnership. It was agreed in 2014–2015 after work that began in 2007.
- It covers about 29,000 ha at two sites with Secwepemctsín names: Ne-sextsine (Flat Rock) and Peskwenkwinem (Potato Mountain). The AAC is about 40,000 m³/yr.
- The community forest worked with Williams Lake First Nation (WLFN) and the Cariboo Fire Centre on **cultural and prescribed burning**. WLFN has treated about 1,400 ha around Williams Lake.

— [UBC Wiki: Williams Lake CF governance](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:FRST370/The_Williams_Lake_Community_Forest,_British_Columbia,_Canada:_history,_governance_and_lessons_learned); [BC gov 2014 release](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2014FLNR0018-000272); [Williams Lake CF](https://williamslakecommunityforest.com/); [Williams Lake Tribune: cultural and prescribed burning](https://www.wltribune.com/local-news/bringing-back-indigenous-practices-in-bc-wildfire-management-8034115); [BCWS: cultural burn within WLFN](https://blog.gov.bc.ca/bcwildfire/cultural-burn-planned-within-williams-lake-first-nation-2/)

**Burns Lake Community Forest**
- Shareholders: Wet'suwet'en First Nation, Ts'il Kaz Koh First Nation and the Village of Burns Lake, "with acknowledgement to the Office of the Wet'suwet'en."
- A six-member board: three reserved First Nation seats (Ts'il Kaz Koh, Wet'suwet'en First Nation, Office of the Wet'suwet'en Hereditary Chiefs) and three community-at-large members appointed by the Village.
- It is run through Comfor Management Services Ltd.
- The brief's "six First Nations" framing is not supported by these snippets. The current structure names two elected-band First Nations plus the Hereditary Chiefs' office.

— [Burns Lake CF: Team](https://blcomfor.com/team/); [Village of Burns Lake: board recruitment, Apr 2026](https://www.burnslake.ca/community/news/2026-04-14-burns-lake-community-forest-seeking-board-members)

**Likely–Xatśūll Community Forest (Cariboo)**
- The Forests Minister invited the Likely–Xatśūll Community Forest and the Big Lake Community Association to apply for a CFA of about **20,000 m³ AAC over 25 years**, with 10,000 m³ to each partner, "that could be combined and used jointly in a partnership."
- Xatśūll (Soda Creek) is a Northern Secwépemc First Nation.

— [Williams Lake Tribune](https://www.wltribune.com/news/likely-xatsull-and-big-lake-invited-to-apply-for-community-forest-partnership/); [BC Rural Centre: Likely Xatsull CF (PDF)](https://bcruralcentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Likely-Xatsull-Community-Forest.pdf)

**Esk'etemc**
- Esk'etemc (Alkali Lake) is a Secwépemc community that manages resources through its own company, Alkali Resource Management Ltd. (ARM).
- I did not confirm a community-forest partnership in these results.

— [Wikipedia: Esk'etemc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esk%CA%BCetemc)

**First Nations Woodland Licence (FNWL)**
- "an area-based, long-term forest tenure unique to First Nations." Terms start at 25 years.
- It gives exclusive timber-harvest rights, rights to manage and charge for botanical forest products, and room for "Aboriginal stewardship." A management plan is required.
- The largest FNWL to date was awarded to Lheidli T'enneh in 2022.

— [Province of BC: FNWL](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/first-nations-woodland-licence); [FNWL Information Guide (PDF)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/first-nations-woodland-licence/first_nations_woodland_licence_information_guide.pdf); [BC gov 2022: largest FNWL](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2022FOR0046-001110)

### Inferences
- Governance options that are well documented and could become student choices in the Partnership round:
  - equal board seats (Cheakamus)
  - 50/50 limited-partnership ownership (Williams Lake)
  - reserved seats for elected and hereditary governance (Burns Lake)
  - separate volumes that partners combine (Likely–Xatśūll)
  - a separate First Nation tenure (FNWL)
- Well-supported outcomes to model:
  - lower harvest levels with ecosystem-based management (Cheakamus)
  - cultural or prescribed burning for wildfire risk (Williams Lake)
  - long planning horizons (25+ year tenures)
  - local economic benefits (BCCFA figures)

## 5. Guidance for non-Indigenous educators presenting Indigenous governance in a role-play

### Takeaway
The **First Peoples Principles of Learning (FPPL)** from the First Nations Education Steering Committee (FNESC) are the recognized BC framework. FNESC resources stress authentic, locally sourced content and working through appropriate local organizations. **I found no explicit FNESC rule on students role-playing a specific nation.** The recommendations below are inferences from those principles plus common practice, and are marked as such. (Medium confidence.)

### Cited Findings
- The FPPL were articulated by Indigenous Elders, scholars and knowledge keepers for the English First Peoples course created by the BC Ministry of Education and FNESC in 2006/07. — [FNESC: First Peoples Principles of Learning](https://www.fnesc.ca/first-peoples-principles-of-learning/); [FPPL poster (PDF)](https://www.fnesc.ca/wp/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/PUB-LFP-POSTER-Principles-of-Learning-First-Peoples-poster-11x17.pdf); [BC gov FPPL (PDF)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/kindergarten-to-grade-12/teach/teaching-tools/aboriginal-education/principles_of_learning.pdf)
- One principle: "Learning involves recognizing that some knowledge is sacred and only shared with permission and/or in certain situations." — [FNESC FPPL](https://www.fnesc.ca/first-peoples-principles-of-learning/)
- FNESC teacher resource guides advise working with appropriate local organizations. They also note it is important that particular Elders and knowledge keepers "do not get over-worked or called upon too often." — [FNESC Learning First Peoples resources](https://www.fnesc.ca/learningfirstpeoples/); [FNESC Grade 10–12 Teacher Resource Guide (PDF)](https://www.fnesc.ca/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/PUBLICATION-LFP-EFP-10-12-FINAL-2018-08-13-1.pdf)
- SD6 already has Indigenous Education Support Workers and agreements with the two First Nations. These are the natural local contacts for reviewing the materials. — [SD6 Indigenous Education](https://www.sd6.bc.ca/programs-and-services/indigenous-education)

### Inferences (not directly sourced; for the facilitator)
- **Do not have students play the Shuswap Band, Ktunaxa or Métis.** Use a generic, clearly fictional "First Nation partner government." Alternatively, keep the nation's role outside student control, e.g. a facilitator card reading "The Nation's Chief and Council have stated…", written only from published public statements such as "stewardship comes before economics."
- **Frame the nation as a government and rights holder, not a stakeholder.** Show partnership as government-to-government, as in Golden's three-government MOU.
- **Avoid inventing cultural practices, ceremonies or "traditional knowledge" for the game** (FPPL: some knowledge is sacred). Refer to real precedents such as cultural burning only through the sources cited above.
- **Avoid pitting nations against each other** or having students "decide" overlapping territory.
- **Before running the round, ask SD6 Indigenous Education or MNCRS to review the materials**, and cite Kenpesq't as a real, ongoing local process.
- **Use the local acknowledgement wording** from Section 1 rather than writing a new one.

### Gaps
- I did not find explicit FNESC or BC Ministry guidance on simulations or role-play (full PDFs not searched). The role-play recommendations above are inference, not a quoted policy.
