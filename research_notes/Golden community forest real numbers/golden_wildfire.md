## 2. FireSmart activity, Selkirk Hill treatments, grants, treatment costs

### Takeaway
Hands-on fuel reduction has happened at **Selkirk Heights** (the Selkirk Hill area). BC Wildfire Service unit crews removed danger trees and surface fuels there over June 17–20 of a year I could not confirm, probably 2013. The work was part of a local Wildfire Prevention Program started in summer 2012. Area A (with Area B) received a $182,400 Columbia Basin grant for FireSmart home assessments and rebates. BC fuel-treatment costs commonly run about **$3,000–$10,000+/ha**.

### Cited Findings
- A BC Forest Protection Branch / GAEMP partnership sent unit crews from the Columbia Fire Zone (Southeast Fire Centre) to **Selkirk Heights** from **June 17 to 20**. They removed dangerous trees and reduced natural fuel on the forest floor. The work continued the **Wildfire Prevention Program launched in summer 2012**, aiming to "limit the rapid spread of fire" and "allow faster access for initial attack crews" [Med on content; year not shown in the snippet] — [Golden Star, "Wildfire mitigation project comes to Golden"](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/wildfire-mitigation-project-comes-to-golden/)
- CSRD Electoral Areas A (Rural Golden) and B (Rural Revelstoke) are receiving **$182,400** through the **Columbia Basin Wildfire Resiliency Initiative**. The grant pilots an expanded FireSmart home assessment and rebate program for vulnerable residents [Med; date not shown, likely 2025–26] — [Golden Star, "CSRD: Grant supports wildfire preparedness in Area A"](https://thegoldenstar.net/local-news/csrd-grant-supports-wildfire-preparedness/)

## 3. Local fire history near Golden

### Takeaway
The defining recent event is the **2024 Dogtooth wildfire** (N21427) south of Golden near Parson. It burned about 5,680 ha, destroyed at least six homes on Rauch Road, put more than 100 properties under evacuation order and about 1,044 under alert. **2026 Sea Lion Mountain** (N41412), about 27 km northeast in the Blaeberry area, reached about 1,144 ha with no threat to structures. On Mount 7 itself, the only fire found was a small, lightning-caused fire (N41279, "Kicking Horse River") that stayed at essentially 0 ha.

## 4. Costs of interface fires and prescribed / cultural burning; prescribed burns near Golden

### Takeaway
Provincial suppression costs run $600M to more than $1B in bad years. The 2018 Abbott-Chapman review urged a shift toward prevention, prescribed burning and partnership with First Nations. I found no source for per-hectare prescribed-burn costs in BC or for any prescribed burns near Golden.

### Cited Findings
- *Addressing the New Normal: 21st Century Disaster Management in BC* (Abbott & Chapman, submitted **April 30, 2018**) made **108 recommendations**. They include prioritizing proactive prevention, prescribed burning, and better fire-management coordination with First Nations. The Province answered with an Action Plan on Oct 31, 2018 [High] — [Report PDF](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/public-safety-and-emergency-services/emergency-preparedness-response-recovery/embc/bc-flood-and-wildfire-review-addressing-the-new-normal-21st-century-disaster-management-in-bc-web.pdf); [BC emergency management reports](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/emergency-management/emergency-management/reports)
- In 2021 the report's authors said that its warnings had largely gone unheeded [Med] — [CBC 2021](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-wildfires-2018-review-warnings-1.6105778)
- There is US economics literature comparing thinning with prescribed fire costs — [Chalmers, "Thinning and prescribed fire as methods to reduce fuel" (FRAMES)](https://www.frames.gov/documents/ffs/ffs017_chalmers_economics.pdf)
- The Southeast Fire Centre / Columbia Fire Zone is the BCWS unit responsible for Golden [High] — [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/wildfire-mitigation-project-comes-to-golden/)

### Inferences
- Prescribed burning is generally cheaper per hectare than mechanical thinning once a site is prepared. Treat that as a general statement only: I did not find a BC-specific figure.

## 5. Fire-behaviour facts for students

### Takeaway
There is good published support for the core teaching points: suppression lets fuels build up, and thinning plus burning reduce fire intensity. The Golden-specific ecology is weakly sourced here. Golden sits in the Rocky Mountain Trench, where drier forest types meet the interior cedar-hemlock (ICH) forest.

### Cited Findings
- Fire suppression makes later wildfires more severe. It adds to the effects of climate change and fuel build-up by removing the frequent low-severity fires that once thinned forests [High; peer-reviewed simulation study] — [Kreider et al. 2024, *Nature Communications* (PMC10963776)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10963776/)
- Integrated fire management, which combines prescribed and cultural burning with suppression, is proposed as an adaptation strategy for altered fire regimes [High] — [Communications Earth & Environment 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-025-02165-9)
- Indigenous fire management as a conceptual model [High] — [Ecology and Society 25(4):11](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol25/iss4/art11/)
- A fuel break is a strip where vegetation is reduced, thinned or removed to break the continuity of burnable material. Fuel treatments aim to lower surface fuels, raise the canopy base (by pruning ladder fuels) and space out crowns to prevent crown fire [Med] — [Selkirk College, "Understanding fuel treatments to mitigate wildfire risk"](https://selkirk.ca/news/understanding-fuel-treatments-mitigate-wildfire-risk); [BCWS Fuel Management Prescription Guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/public-safety-and-emergency-services/wildfire-status/prevention/fire-fuel-management/fuels-management/23_fuel_management_prescription_guidance_v2.pdf)
- The Selkirk Heights project's stated rationale was that reducing forest-floor fuels and danger trees limits rapid fire spread and improves access for initial attack crews [Med] — [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/wildfire-mitigation-project-comes-to-golden/)
- The BC government keeps a fuel-management references list — [BC fuel management references and literature](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/safety/wildfire-status/prevention/fire-fuel-management/fuel-management/references-and-literature)

### Inferences
- Classroom framing: dry, open forests used to burn often and at low intensity. A century of putting fires out filled in the understory with small trees ("ladder fuels") and dead wood, so fires now climb into the crowns. Thinning removes those ladders and spaces out the crowns. A follow-up prescribed burn clears the surface fuels. Treatments have to be repeated about every 10–20 years or the fuel returns. This matches the cited literature in outline, but the repeat interval is **not** sourced here.
- Wetter ICH forests historically had less frequent, more severe fires, so suppression has changed them less. Say this as a general ecological point only. It is not sourced here and should be verified before students see it.

### Gaps
- A Golden-specific breakdown of biogeoclimatic zones (IDF / MS / ICH) and fuel types (for example C-3, C-7, M-2) on the Selkirk Hill and Mount 7 slopes. This would be in the CWRP, which could not be accessed.
