## 1. Current Golden TSA allowable annual cut (AAC), determination and partitions

### Takeaway
The most recent Golden TSA AAC I could find is **485,000 m3/year, effective June 3, 2010**. It was "fully partitioned to conventional resources," meaning there is no species or area partition. I found no newer determination (2020-2026). The Forest Act lets the chief forester postpone a determination to at most 15 years after the last one, so a new determination was legally due around June 2025. I could not confirm whether one has been made.

### Cited Findings
- "The Golden TSA allowable annual cut (AAC) is 485,000 cubic metres as of June 3, 2010." — [BC Gov, Golden TSA page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/timber-supply-review-and-allowable-annual-cut/allowable-annual-cut-timber-supply-areas/golden-tsa) (search snippet; high confidence for the 2010 figure).
- The Golden TSA is in the Southern Interior Forest Region. As of March 31, 2010 its AAC was 485,000 m3, "fully partitioned to conventional resources." The 2010 report covers apportionment to forest licences, BCTS timber sale licences, community forest agreements and forest service reserves, and lists commitments to Downie Timber Ltd. and Louisiana-Pacific Canada Ltd. — [Golden TSA AAC, Apportionment, and Commitments - 2010 (Canada Commons copy of BC Gov report)](https://canadacommons.ca/artifacts/29809008/golden-tsa-aac-apportionment-and-commitments/30708853/) (search snippet; I could not see the category volumes).
- The last review cycle was Timber Supply Review #4, whose data package and analysis report were published c. 2008-2009 — [Golden TSA TSR #4 Data Package](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/stewardship/forest-analysis-inventory/tsr-annual-allowable-cut/golden_tsa_data_package.pdf); [Golden TSA Timber Supply Analysis Report](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/stewardship/forest-analysis-inventory/tsr-annual-allowable-cut/golden_tsa_analysis_report.pdf).
- Under Forest Act s. 8(3.1), the chief forester may postpone the next determination by written order to a date up to 15 years after the last one, with written reasons — [BC Gov, AAC - Timber Supply Areas](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/timber-supply-review-and-allowable-annual-cut/allowable-annual-cut-timber-supply-areas).
- Current apportionment reports exist for 2026-27, for example the Apportionment System "Linkages and Licences" report — [BC Gov aptr041 2026-27](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/apportionment/2026-27/aptr041_linkageslicences.pdf) (could not open, so no Golden numbers).

### Inferences
- For the simulation, 485,000 m3/yr is the defensible "current published" Golden TSA AAC. Label it "(2010 determination; newer determination not confirmed as of Sept 2026)."
- 2010 + 15 years = 2025, so either a new rationale was issued in 2025-26 that search engines have not indexed, or the review is overdue or was handled under 2025-26 Forest Act changes. Check the Golden TSA page directly before publishing.
- The chief forester in 2010 was most likely Jim Snetsinger (Chief Forester c. 2004-2012). This comes from background knowledge, not a fetched source, so it is **unverified**.

### Gaps
- I could not open the 2010 rationale PDF, so I have no confirmed author signature or exact effective date wording.
- I have no current apportionment breakdown in m3 for BCTS, replaceable forest licences, First Nations and forest service reserve, because the gov.bc.ca apportionment reports were blocked.
- I found no evidence of a 2020-2026 AAC determination or a postponement order for Golden.

## 2. Was ~4% of the local AAC ever set aside for a Golden community forest?

### Takeaway
**Yes, largely confirmed.** In **2006 the forests minister allocated 20,000 m3/yr** for a Golden-area community forest. In **2010 the province reallocated that 20,000 m3 to BC Timber Sales**, and no community forest agreement was ever issued. 20,000 m3 is **about 4.1% of the 485,000 m3 AAC**, which matches the local's recollection of "roughly 4%." A new effort, the **Kenpesq't Community Forest** (Shuswap Band + Town of Golden + CSRD Area A, MOU signed Nov 21, 2022), is under way. As of my searches it did not hold a tenure.

### Cited Findings
- "There have been efforts to bring a community forest to Golden and the surrounding area dating back to 1999. In 2006 the forest minister allocated 20,000 cubic metres of timber volume towards a community forest for the Golden area, but in 2010 the provincial government reallocated the 20,000 cubic metre volume to BC Timber Sales." — [Golden & Area Community Forest Initiative, About](https://goldenareacf.com/about); same history in [The Golden Star, "Bringing a community forest to Golden"](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/bringing-a-community-forest-to-golden/) and republished by [BC Community Forest Association](https://bccfa.ca/bringing-a-community-forest-to-golden/) (search snippets; three independent-looking outlets carrying the same text, so medium-high confidence. I could not get the article date or author).
- The Shuswap Band, the Town of Golden and CSRD Area A are working together on a new community forest tenure around Golden, within Shuswap Band traditional territory, to be called the **Kenpesq't Community Forest** ("Kenpesq't" is the Secwepemctsin name for the Shuswap Band) — [Town of Golden, Kenpesq't Community Forest](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest).
- On **November 21, 2022**, at the Shuswap Band office near Invermere, the three governments signed an MOU reaffirming their intent to create the Kenpesq't Community Forest "within the Golden Timber Supply Area" — [Town of Golden, Kenpesq't Community Forest](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/kenpesqt-community-forest).
- The process is led by the Golden & Area Community Forest Team (GACFT), with representatives of all three communities — [Golden & Area Community Forest Initiative](https://goldenareacf.com/); [Town of Golden, Golden and Area Community Forest Initiative](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/golden-and-area-community-forest-initiative).
- Related: The Golden Star reported that the Shuswap Band submitted a request for partnership and collaboration with the CSRD — [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/shuswap-band-submits-request-for-partnership-collaboration-with-csrd/) (date not retrieved).
- Context on CFAs: they are area-based licences managed by a local government, community group, First Nation or combination, run for community benefit, with terms of 25-99 years and replaceable every 10 years — [BC Gov, Community Forest Agreements](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/community-forest-agreements).
- Provincial context in 2026: government news releases continue to invite community forest expansions of similar size, for example McLeod Lake Mackenzie Community Forest invited to expand by ~10,400 ha "which includes 20,000 cubic metres of wood fibre annually" — [BC Gov News 2026FOR0047](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0047-001063). A Golden Chamber event in June 2026 presented the "New Forest Act" — [Golden Chamber of Commerce](https://www.goldenchamber.bc.ca/2026/05/26/new-forest-act-june-2026/).

### Inferences
- 20,000 / 485,000 = 4.12%. Caveat: the 2006 allocation was made against the pre-2010 AAC, which I did not find. If that AAC differed from 485,000, the 2006 share was not exactly 4.1%. It was still certainly in the "roughly 4%" range if the earlier AAC was similar.
- The 2006 volume very likely came from the Forestry Revitalization Plan (2003) "take-back" of ~20% of major-licensee replaceable volume. That pool was redistributed to BCTS, First Nations and community forests/woodlots, and undesignated take-back volume was later moved to BCTS. This fits the 2010 reallocation to BCTS. Inference only: I found no source tying Golden's 20,000 m3 specifically to the take-back.
- Suggested classroom framing: "A Golden community forest was once promised 20,000 m3 a year (about 4% of the Golden TSA's 485,000 m3 cut) in 2006. That volume went to BC Timber Sales in 2010 before a licence was issued. A new partnership (Kenpesq't) has been working on it since 2022."

### Gaps
- I have not confirmed whether the Kenpesq't initiative has received a provincial invitation to apply, a proposed volume, or a CFA as of Sept 2026, because the Town page and goldenareacf.com could not be opened.
- I found no primary 2006 ministerial release or 2010 reallocation decision document, and no e-KNOW or Columbia Valley Pioneer coverage.

## 4. Timber value: stumpage, log prices, community forest net revenue per m3

### Takeaway
BC's Interior average stumpage is forecast at about **$13.55-$13.83/m3** for 2026/27. I **could not retrieve** the Interior Log Market Report price tables for delivered log prices. I also found **no sourced figure for community forest net revenue per m3**. A defensible classroom range is given below as an inference.

### Cited Findings
- **Interior average stumpage** is forecast at **$13.83/m3** for fiscal 2026/27 in the Province's First Quarterly Report (Sept 2026) — [BC Gov First Quarterly Report, Sept 2026](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/quarterly-reports/2026-27_first_quarterly_sept_2026_web_secured.pdf) (search snippet; medium confidence, I did not read the table).
- An earlier forecast put Crown stumpage at **$13.55/m3**, "11 per cent below February's budget" — [Wood Central](https://woodcentral.com.au/bc-stumpage-rates-forecast/) (snippet; the exact fiscal year and the Interior-vs-provincial split are unclear).
- One snippet said BC Timber Sales obtained an average **$37.33/m3**, compared with **$13.32/m3** collected from area-based tenures. The year and context were unclear, and the aggregator was unclear. Low confidence — see search results around [Focus on Victoria, "Forestry doesn't pay the bills"](https://www.focusonvictoria.ca/issue-analysis/35/).
- The BC Interior Log Market Report (Oct 1 - Dec 31, 2025) reports arm's-length Interior log purchases in $/m3 by species (spruce, pine, balsam fir, Douglas-fir, cedar, hemlock) — [BC Gov 3-month Interior Log Market Report Dec 2025](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-pricing/interior-timber-pricing/interior-log-market-reports/3mi_dec_25.pdf) (exists; I could not retrieve the prices).
- BCCFA 2026 Indicators Report (released Sept 2026): 37 reporting community forests (77% of operating members) generated **>$129 million** in economic activity in 2025, and 1,833 people earned all or part of their income from community forest activities — [Tree Frog Creative summary](https://treefrogcreative.ca/bc-community-forest-association-2026-indicators-report-measuring-the-benefits-of-community-forestry/); [102.9 Rewind Radio (East Kootenay), 2026-09-17](https://1029rewindradio.ca/2026/09/17/new-report-shows-more-than-129m-generated-from-b-c-community-forests-in-2025/). I did not retrieve per-m3 or net-income figures.

### Inferences (estimates, not sourced figures)
- **Delivered Interior log prices:** I could not verify a figure. From general background knowledge (not verified this session), recent Interior arm's-length sawlog prices for spruce, pine and fir have generally been about **$80-$130/m3 delivered**. Cedar is often higher and pulp/low-grade logs much lower. Treat this as a placeholder until someone reads the 3mi_dec_25 PDF.
- **Community forest net revenue:** a CF's net is roughly log revenue minus harvesting, hauling, road, silviculture, planning and admin costs, minus stumpage (CFAs pay tabular stumpage, generally lower than appraised rates). As a rough classroom range, **about $5-$20 net per m3 harvested**, with **~$10/m3** as a round central value. This comes from reasoning and general knowledge, **not a sourced statistic**. Losses happen in weak lumber markets or on steep, high-cost ground like much of the Golden TSA.
- **Illustrative simulation numbers:** 20,000 m3/yr × $10/m3 ≈ **$200,000/yr net** (range roughly $100k-$400k). Gross log sales at ~$100/m3 would be about $2 million/yr. Provincial stumpage at ~$14/m3 would be about $280,000/yr, but a CFA would pay tabular rates, likely less.

- No verified BCCFA per-m3 net-income indicator.
- No Golden/Selkirk-specific stumpage table (the Interior Non-Appraised Tabular Stumpage Rates page was blocked).

## 5. Main tenure holders in the Golden TSA and recent changes

### Takeaway
The historic major licensees were **Louisiana-Pacific** (Golden engineered-wood mill) and **Downie Timber Ltd.**, alongside **BC Timber Sales (Okanagan-Columbia business area)**. LP's Golden forest licence(s) and mill passed to **Pacific Woodtech (PWT)** in a deal that closed Aug 1, 2022. Sources disagree on the date (2022 vs 2024). Canfor operates in the adjacent Invermere/Radium area and was not identified as a Golden TSA licensee in my sources.

### Cited Findings
- The Golden TSA DFAM group was made up of **Louisiana-Pacific, Downie Timber Ltd., and BC Timber Sales (Okanagan Columbia)** (TSR #4 data package, c. 2008) — [Golden TSA TSR #4 Data Package](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/stewardship/forest-analysis-inventory/tsr-annual-allowable-cut/golden_tsa_data_package.pdf).
- 2010 commitments list volumes for **Downie Timber Ltd.** and **Louisiana-Pacific Canada Ltd.** — [Golden TSA AAC, Apportionment, and Commitments - 2010](https://canadacommons.ca/artifacts/29809008/golden-tsa-aac-apportionment-and-commitments/30708853/).
- "On August 1, 2022, Pacific Woodtech (PWT) closed on the sale of Louisiana-Pacific's Engineered Wood Products business, which included three forest licences in the Okanagan and Golden Timber Supply Areas… representing a total [AAC] of 487,710 m3" (combined across both TSAs; the Golden share was not given) — [BC Gov tenure transfer notice, Oct 12, 2022](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/transfers/lp_to_pwt_transfer_info.pdf). PWT presented to the CSRD on Dec 9, 2022 — [CSRD document](https://pub-csrd.escribemeetings.com/FileStream.ashx?DocumentId=31697).
- The Golden Star reported that "Pacific Woodtech assumed ownership of the Golden mill effective August 1, 2024," with LP providing transition services — [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/pacific-woodtech-assumes-ownership-of-golden-mill/). **Conflict:** the BC Gov notice gives Aug 1, 2022. The "2024" in the search summary may be a mis-rendering, so treat 2022 (from the government notice) as more reliable.

### Inferences
- The likely current major players are **Pacific Woodtech** (ex-LP licence and Golden mill), **Downie Timber** (Revelstoke-based, historic Golden licence), and **BCTS**. BCTS's share probably includes the 20,000 m3 reallocated in 2010. None of this is confirmed with 2026 apportionment numbers.

### Gaps
- No 2026 volumes per licensee.
- Unknown whether Downie still holds its Golden licence.
- No details on First Nations tenures (for example Shuswap Band/Kenpesq't Forestry or Ktunaxa) in the Golden TSA.
