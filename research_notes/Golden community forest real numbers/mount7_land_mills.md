# Mount 7 (Golden, BC): land, forest, recreation, harvesting, and Golden's mills

Research date: 2026-09-27. Method note: direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy for every domain tried (goldencyclingclub.com, www2.gov.bc.ca, thegoldenstar.net, golden.ca). All findings below come from search-engine result text for the cited URLs, not from reading the full documents. Treat exact figures as "as reported in the search result for that URL". Anyone checking should open the GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF (Nov 22, 2023) first, since it holds most of the land-status detail.

## 1. Land status and tenure of Mount 7's lower and mid slopes above Selkirk Hill / Town of Golden

### Takeaway
Mount 7 is a patchwork. Most of the mountain is provincial Crown land, where Recreation Sites and Trails BC (RSTBC) manages recreation under FRPA. The lower slopes, "below 5K" (the 5 km mark on the Mount 7 Forest Service Road), include several parcels owned by the Town of Golden and one private parcel. Timber tenures are active on the mountain: a woodlot licence, a Timber Sale Licence (TSL), and a small-scale permit. The Golden Cycling Club (GCC) manages the trails under a 2023 trail plan, working with the Town, the private landowner, Indigenous groups and RSTBC.

### Cited Findings
- Mount 7 is a mountain in the Canadian Rockies just southeast of Golden, BC. It is named for the "7" snow formation near its peak. — [Wikipedia: Mount 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_7)
- Access is by a roughly 15 km unpaved forestry road (Mount 7 FSR) to the lookout and paragliding launch. High-clearance 4x4 is recommended. — [Golden Hikes](https://goldenhikes.ca/trail/mount-7/); [Trailforks Mount 7 trail](https://www.trailforks.com/trails/mount-7-trail-to-peak/)
- The GCC "Mount 7 Trail Plan" was submitted to the GCC on November 22, 2023. It says RSTBC manages recreation on BC Crown land under the Forest and Range Practices Act (FRPA), and that Chapter 10 of the Recreation Manual governs recreation trail management. — [GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)
- According to the trail plan, the lower portion of Mount 7 below 5K contains one private parcel and several municipal parcels owned by the Town of Golden. — [GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)
- One private parcel crosses the Mount 7 trail network. The GCC has permission from the landowner, whose conditions include no machine-built trail construction. — [GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)
- According to the trail plan, the Mount 7 area has an active woodlot licence, a Timber Sale Licence (TSL), and a small-scale permit. — [GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)
- How woodlot licences work: the holder gets exclusive rights to manage and harvest Crown timber in the licence area. The maximum Crown area is 1,200 ha in the Interior, and a woodlot licence may include private land. — [BC Gov: Woodlot Licence](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/forest-tenures/timber-harvesting-rights/woodlot-licence)
- The GCC maintains "four networks of multi-use trails", more than 180 km in total, including Mount 7. It works with the Town of Golden, private land managers, Indigenous groups, the local community and RSTBC. — [GCC trails page](https://goldencyclingclub.com/golden-cycling-club-trails/); [GCC home](https://goldencyclingclub.com/)
- The Town of Golden is replacing its OCP. The new plan is Official Community Plan Bylaw No. 1516, 2026, which went to public hearing around June 2026. It replaces OCP Bylaw No. 1222, 2008. Search text says the OCP includes policies to keep and expand tree cover on steep slopes to limit erosion and natural hazards. It also says Selkirk Hill's ageing infrastructure limits further development until upgrades are done. — [OCP Bylaw 1516, 2026 (public hearing PDF)](https://www.golden.ca/sites/default/files/2026-06/Bylaw%201516%202026%20Town%20of%20Golden%20Bylaw%20Official%20Community%20Plan%20PH.pdf); [2026 OCP project page](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/current-projects/2026-official-community-plan); [OCP 2008 PDF](https://www.golden.ca/sites/default/files/2022-01/Bylaw-No-1222,-2008-Official-Community-Plan.pdf)
- The paragliding launch sits at the top of the Mount 7 road and is shared with the old downhill race start. In Sept 2022, RCMP briefly closed all Mount 7 bike trails and the paragliding launch pad during an investigation. — [Tourism Golden: Mount 7](https://www.tourismgolden.com/places/mount-7); [Golden Star, 2022](https://www.thegoldenstar.net/news/police-incident-shuts-down-mt-7-mountain-biking-trails-paraglide-launch-4031054)

### Inferences
- The most realistic picture for "the lower slopes right above the neighbourhood" is a mix of Town-owned land near the base (which a municipality could manage through a community forest agreement, as a park, or as interface fuel-management land) and provincial Crown land further up, already covered by third-party timber tenures (woodlot, TSL). That makes the real tenure story a good fit for a simulation where several players hold rights to the same hillside.
- The Columbia Woodlot Association carried out the wildfire mulching on Mount 7 (see section 3). The woodlot mentioned in the trail plan is probably a woodlot licence held by one of its members, but this is not confirmed.
- Selkirk Hill is a residential area of Golden at the foot of Mount 7. This is background knowledge; I could not open a source that places it exactly.

### Gaps
- I could not find the woodlot licence number or holder, the TSL holder (probably BC Timber Sales), or their locations relative to the trails. The trail plan's maps would show them, and iMapBC / the BC Woodlot Licence district list would confirm.
- I could not confirm whether the Mount 7 trails are formally authorized under FRPA s.57 or established as an RSTBC recreation trail under s.56, whether a Mount 7 Recreation Site exists, or what tenure (if any) covers the paragliding launch.
- I could not confirm the exact Town boundary on Mount 7 or the OCP / CSRD land-use designations for those slopes. The full OCP text could not be opened.
- I found nothing linking "Mount 7 Lodge(s)" or the Trans Canada Trail to land tenure on the mountain. Mount 7 Lodges is a tourist accommodation listing ([Super, Natural BC](https://www.supernaturalbc.com/listings/mount-7-lodges-4541544/)).

## 2. Forest types (BEC zones, species) and insects

### Takeaway
The valley-bottom and lower-slope forests around Golden fall mainly in the ICHmk1 subzone (Kootenay Moist Cool Interior Cedar–Hemlock). Because of past wildfires, much of it is seral lodgepole pine with Douglas-fir and western larch at low elevations. The main bark beetles in the Golden TSA are Douglas-fir beetle, mountain pine beetle, spruce beetle and western balsam bark beetle. I found no Mount 7-specific stand or beetle data.

### Cited Findings
- ICHmk1 occurs in the Rocky Mountain Trench between the Spillimacheen and Blaeberry rivers (Golden lies in that stretch) and along the Kicking Horse River. Its elevation range is about 800–1,550 m on south aspects and 750–1,500 m on north aspects. Summers are warm and wet, winters cool with moderate snowfall. Lodgepole pine seral stands are common because of widespread wildfires, with Douglas-fir and western larch at low elevations. — [BC Ministry of Forests LMH20 (Field guide, Nelson Forest Region)](https://www.for.gov.bc.ca/hfd/pubs/docs/lmh/LMH20-2.pdf)
- The main bark beetles in the Golden TSA are spruce beetle, Douglas-fir beetle and mountain pine beetle. Western balsam bark beetle causes chronic mortality, with attack area of 11,985 ha in 2022 and 11,798 ha in 2023. — [Selkirk Natural Resource District, Golden TSA Forest Health Strategy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/forest-health/fh-strategies/goldenfhstrategy.pdf)
- The Golden TSA runs along the Rocky Mountain Trench and upper Columbia to the Big Bend near Mica Dam. Its AAC was 485,000 m³ as of June 3, 2010, per the search result, which may be out of date. — [BC Gov: Golden TSA](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/timber-supply-review-and-allowable-annual-cut/allowable-annual-cut-timber-supply-areas/golden-tsa)
- Douglas-fir is the main species for the Golden LVL plant. The first LVL line (1997) was described as "primarily utilizing Douglas fir". — [Kootenay Business](https://kootenaybiz.com/golden/article/a_main_player_in_golden)
- The Columbia Woodlot Association described Mount 7 as a high-use recreation area and a likely point of human-caused ignition, and treated it for ground-fire risk. — [e-Know](https://www.e-know.ca/regions/east-kootenay/woodlots-bc-to-lead-wildfire-risk-reduction-efforts/)

### Inferences
- The lower, south- and west-facing slopes of Mount 7 above town are dry and in the wildland-urban interface. A realistic simulation forest would be Douglas-fir-dominated, mixed with lodgepole pine and western larch, possibly some ponderosa-type dry sites, with spruce and subalpine fir higher up (ESSF near the launch at about 1,900+ m). The ESSF and upper-slope composition is inference from general Trench ecology, not from a Mount 7-specific source.
- Douglas-fir beetle is the most plausible insect threat for a lower-slope Douglas-fir stand. Mountain pine beetle affects the pine component.

### Gaps
- I found no BEC map extract specific to Mount 7. Whether IDF or ICHxw pockets occur on the lowest slopes, and where the ICH/ESSF transition sits, is unconfirmed.
- I found no documented beetle outbreak specific to Mount 7.

## 3. Harvesting, fuel treatments, logging and controversy on Mount 7 (last ~30 years)

### Takeaway
The mountain is a working landscape. The main access road is a logging road, and a woodlot, a TSL and a small-scale permit are active there. The best-documented recent work is wildfire fuel treatment by the Columbia Woodlot Association: an 11 ha machine-mulching project (Phase 1), then $271,500 in provincial funding for "Mount 7 Phase 2" announced in June 2026. I found no reported public controversy over cutblocks on Mount 7.

### Cited Findings
- The Mount 7 road to the launch is described as a logging road (Mount 7 Forest Service Road). — [Trailforks](https://www.trailforks.com/trails/mount-7-trail-to-peak/); [Tourism Golden blog](https://www.tourismgolden.com/blog/adventuring-mount-7)
- The trail plan reports an active woodlot licence, TSL and small-scale permit in the Mount 7 area (Nov 2023). — [GCC Mount 7 Trail Plan PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/GCC-Mt.-7-Trail-Plan-Final-compressed-compressed.pdf)
- The Columbia Woodlot Association did a wildfire risk reduction treatment on Mount 7 using machine mulching over 11 hectares. A single machine cuts, grinds and clears vegetation, then spreads the mulch to slow ground-fire spread. FESBC and Columbia Basin Trust funding supported a mulching research project on how well mulching works in the WUI. The Woodlots BC program as a whole got up to $1.7M from FESBC over two fiscal years, aiming to treat about 400 ha. The article date was not visible; it is probably 2023–24. — [e-Know](https://www.e-know.ca/regions/east-kootenay/woodlots-bc-to-lead-wildfire-risk-reduction-efforts/); [FESBC](https://fesbc.ca/woodlots-bc-to-lead-province-wide-wildfire-risk-reduction-efforts-on-woodlots/)
- A 2026 provincial release (2026FOR0031, about June 2026) lists the Columbia Woodlot Association: "$271,500 for Mount 7 Phase 2 Wildfire Risk Reduction Mulching." — [BC Gov News 2026FOR0031-000669](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0031-000669)
- The Town of Golden issued emergency information updates in late July 2024 (e.g., July 29, 2024). The search did not show the topic. It was probably the 2024 wildfire season, but that is unconfirmed. — [Town of Golden notice](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/news-notices/emergency-information-update-july-29-2024-noon)
- For comparison only: a logging controversy near recreation trails is happening on Mount Macpherson near Revelstoke (petition; Wildsight querying industry, May 2026). This is not Mount 7. — [Revelstoke Mountaineer](https://revelstokemountaineer.com/online-petition-seeks-to-halt-mount-macpherson-recreation-area-logging-plans/); [Revelstoke Review, 2026-05-14](https://revelstokereview.com/2026/05/14/wildsight-revelstoke-querying-industry-on-proposed-mount-macpherson-logging/)

### Inferences
- Fuel treatment (thinning and mulching) is currently the most "real" form of harvesting on the lower slopes. A simulation could credibly offer options such as commercial thinning, a fuel break, or a small patch cut.
- The no-machine-construction condition on the private parcel, together with the busy trail network, suggests that trail users and landowners would expect to be consulted about any harvest near the trails. The Revelstoke case shows how that kind of dispute usually plays out in the region.

### Gaps
- I found no news report of a cutblock controversy on Mount 7. That could mean there was none or that search coverage missed it; Golden Star archives could not be opened.
- The volumes, dates and locations of woodlot or TSL harvest on Mount 7 are unknown.

## 4. Golden's mills: history and current status

### Takeaway
Golden's main mill is the veneer / plywood / LVL complex. Evans Forest Products ran it until Louisiana-Pacific bought Evans in 1999. LP sold it to Pacific Woodtech (PWT) on August 1, 2022. The site's sawmill closed in the early 1970s when the site moved into plywood, and plywood production has since stopped "for the time being" under PWT. Employment fell from about 435 direct jobs plus about 200 contractor jobs (2022) to about 350 direct jobs plus about 200 contractor jobs, per a Golden Star article (date not visible, after 2022). The company attributes most of the drop to retirements and attrition.

### Cited Findings
- The Golden site's sawmill stopped operating in the early 1970s when the division expanded into plywood. Commodity and specialty plywood ran until 1997, when the first LVL line (mainly Douglas-fir) was added. A second LVL line came in 1999. — [Kootenay Business](https://kootenaybiz.com/golden/article/a_main_player_in_golden)
- In August 1999, Evans Forest Products was sold to Louisiana-Pacific for $130 million. The deal covered two plants in Golden and a sawmill at Malakwa, west of Revelstoke. Evans had been near bankruptcy in 1996. — [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/forest-company-takeover-1.187318)
- On August 1, 2022, PWT closed its purchase of LP's Engineered Wood Products business. The sale included the Golden LVL and plywood plant plus Red Bluff, CA and Wilmington, NC. The deal was reported at $210M. — [LP investor release](https://investor.lpcorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lp-building-solutions-completes-sale-engineered-wood-products/); [BC Gov transfer info (Oct 12, 2022)](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/transfers/lp_to_pwt_transfer_info.pdf); [Golden Star](https://thegoldenstar.net/news/pacific-woodtech-assumes-ownership-of-golden-mill/); [HBS Dealer](https://hbsdealer.com/pacific-woodtech-completes-acquisition-lps-ewp-business)
- At the 2022 transfer, the Golden operation and its forest tenures employed about 435 local workers and supported about 200 more through contractors (roads, harvesting, hauling, silviculture). — [BC Gov transfer info](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/forestry/timber-tenures/transfers/lp_to_pwt_transfer_info.pdf)
- The Province ran a public-interest review of the transfer of LP's forest tenures to PWT, and the Town of Golden posted notice of it. PWT also presented to the CSRD board on December 9, 2022. — [Town of Golden notice](https://www.golden.ca/town-hall/news-notices/identifying-public-interest-proposed-disposition-louisiana-pacific-canada); [CSRD meeting doc](https://pub-csrd.escribemeetings.com/FileStream.ashx?DocumentId=31697)
- Later, the Golden Star reported the mill employs about 350 workers plus about 200 in contracting and supply. It said most lost jobs came from retirements and attrition, and that Golden is PWT's largest facility "despite no longer producing plywood, at least for the time being". It also reported the mill is hiring general labourers and is one of the town's largest employers. The article date was not visible; it is after 2022 and probably 2024–25. — [Golden Star: "Pacific Woodtech owners optimistic about the future"](https://thegoldenstar.net/local-business/pacific-woodtech-owners-golden-bc/)
- The APA (Engineered Wood Association) lists the mill as a member mill. — [APA MemberMill](https://www.apawood.org/MemberMill?MemberMillID=40)
- For regional context, 2024–25 brought many BC sawmill closures (Canfor Vanderhoof and Fort St. John, West Fraser 100 Mile House, and others), blamed on timber supply, soft markets and US tariffs. None of these were in Golden. — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/canfor-vanderhoof-fort-st-john-sawmill-closure-1.7313849); [West Fraser](https://www.westfraser.com/investors/news/news-releases/west-fraser-reduces-lumber-capacity); [BIV](https://www.biv.com/news/from-new-mines-to-closed-mills-2024-marked-year-of-shake-ups-for-bc-resource-sector-9981601)

### Inferences
- Golden has had no dimension-lumber sawmill of its own since the early 1970s, based on the Kootenay Business history; I found no evidence of a later one. The only large mill is the PWT LVL plant, which uses Douglas-fir veneer logs. That makes the mill a natural buyer for Douglas-fir logs from Mount 7's lower slopes in a simulation.
- The prompt suggested Downie Timber and Canfor. Downie Timber is in Revelstoke and Canfor's nearest mill is at Radium; neither is in Golden. This comes from background knowledge and was not sourced in this session.

### Gaps
- I found no exact date for the "350 workers" article and no 2026 mill news (curtailments, plywood restart). I found no current AAC or licence numbers for the tenures PWT acquired.
- I found no source confirming what the "two plants in Golden" in the 1999 deal were (probably the plywood/veneer plant and the LVL plant).

## 5. Tourism economy relevant to the trails

### Takeaway
Mount 7 is the base of Golden's downhill and shuttle riding and of its paragliding. It hosted the "Psychosis" downhill race from 1998 to 2008, which returned in 2024 for its 25th anniversary. A GCC-commissioned study found about 7,300–7,400 unique mountain bikers in summer 2023, with about $5.8 million in direct spending. 61% were overnight visitors, and 45% came from Alberta.

### Cited Findings
- Summer 2023 Golden Mountain Biking Economic Impact study: more than 7,300 unique riders (just under 7,400) and about $5.8M in direct spending. Riders were 23% residents, 16% day visitors and 61% overnight visitors. The largest origin was Alberta (45%), then Golden residents (23%), and 65% were aged 25–44. The study used 645 on-site interviews plus Trailforks unique-rider data and a KHMR ridership estimate. Riders rated the GCC network high-quality and well maintained. — [GCC Summer 2023 Economic Impact report PDF](https://goldencyclingclub.com/cms/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Summer-2023-Golden-Mountain-Biking-Economic-Impact-Final-Report-updated-May.pdf)
- A 2011 BC Input-Output model report on mountain biking in Rossland and Golden exists as an earlier baseline. — [BC I-O Model Report 2011](http://www.goldenloom.ca/uploads/1/2/0/0/12003480/bc_input-output_model_report-mtnbiking-2011.pdf); see also [ETSI-BC](https://www.etsi-bc.ca/golden-harnesses-the-economic-impact-of-mountain-biking/)
- Golden is one of six pilot communities (with Squamish, Fernie, Kamloops, Vernon and Cumberland) in a Mountain Biking BC community trails impact study. — [Coast Reporter / Squamish Chief](https://www.coastreporter.net/local-latest/dirt-dollars-bc-mountain-biking-study-aims-to-quantify-trail-economics-10145816)
- Mount 7 is Golden's downhill shuttle hotspot and "the heart of local downhill riding for many years". Riders reach it by the FSR or by climbing on a cross-country bike. — [Trailforks: Mount 7 region](https://www.trailforks.com/region/mount-7/); [Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/places/mount-7)
- The Psychosis race ran on Mount 7 from 1998 to 2008 and was billed as the steepest, longest and fastest downhill race. The official record is 12:35.14 (Chris Kovarik, 2008). At the Crankworx Summer Series in 2020, Henry Fitzgerald ran 11:19.19. The race returned under the Psychosis banner on June 22, 2024 for its 25th anniversary. The start shares the steep paraglider launch. — [Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/places/mount-7); [Canadian Cycling Magazine](https://cyclingmagazine.ca/mtb/mt-7-psychosis-returns-at-crankworx-summer-series/); [GCC Psychosis page](https://goldencyclingclub.com/psychosis/); [Mountain Life, 2022](https://www.mountainlifemedia.ca/2022/08/mount-7s-disciples/)
- Visitors watch paragliders, hang gliders and downhill riders launch from the top of Mount 7. — [Tourism Golden](https://www.tourismgolden.com/places/mount-7)
- The GCC network includes Mount 7, Moonraker, CBT and Mountain Shadows, plus the KHMR bike park. — [GCC trails page](https://goldencyclingclub.com/golden-cycling-club-trails/)

### Inferences
- A lower-slope harvest that affects the trails would put some share of about $5.8M a year in rider spending at risk, most of it from Alberta overnight visitors. Mount 7 is only one of several networks, so the at-risk share is less than the whole.

### Gaps
- I found no GCC membership numbers, no Mount 7-specific rider counts, no Kicking Horse Mountain Resort visitor numbers, and no data on paragliding events (e.g., cross-country paragliding competitions or record flights). The earlier "Willi Müller" cross-country competition is from memory only and unverified.
